# Vetting: real engagement, audience fit, brand safety

Load at step 4. Every number here is an **approximate practitioner range (US, 2025–26)**, drawn from published benchmark reports and agency practice. Ranges drift every year — treat them as sanity checks, not facts, and date-stamp every verdict. There is no fake-follower API: all of this is *signals, not verification*. Say so in the roster.

## 0. Which depth, which rubric

| Role | Depth | What decides the verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Reach, affiliate, ambassador (paid) | **Full** | Sections 1–11 |
| Gift-only nano/micro in a seeding wave | **Light**: profile, last 12 posts, one comment pull, a caption scan for safety and competitor tags | Sections 2–4, 7, 8 (stated concern + comment self-descriptions, no audience pull), 9 (captions only), 10 (captions only), 11 — enough to avoid a bad fit at low cost; anyone promoted to paid gets full vetting |
| UGC-for-ads (content for the brand's own ads) | **Full, different rubric** | Section 13 — audience size and fit barely matter |
| LinkedIn voice, newsletter, podcast | **Full, B2B branch** | Section 14 |

## 1. The sample

- The last **12–20 posts** on the platform that matters.
- Leave out: pinned posts, giveaways ("comment to win"), and anything younger than ~48 hours (TikTok, Reels, X) or ~7 days (YouTube). They are missing data, not evidence.
- Score **sponsored** and **organic** posts separately.
- Use the **median**, never the mean. If mean > ~2× median, one viral post is carrying the profile — price and judge on the median and say so.

## 2. Formulas — never compare across them

- **By views** (TikTok, Reels, YouTube, X): (likes + comments + shares + saves) ÷ views, using whatever of those the data exposes. Say which fields were used, and compare only like with like — an ER without shares and saves reads lower than the bands below.
- **By followers** (IG feed images/carousels, LinkedIn): engagements ÷ followers, because views are not public there.
- **View rate**: median views ÷ followers.

## 3. Healthy ranges (approximate)

| Platform | Engagement | View rate | Red line |
|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | ~4–10% by views | nano/micro ~10–50%; mid+ ~5–20% | ER under ~3%, or uniform low views on a big following |
| IG Reels | ~3–8% by views | micro ~20–60%, falling with tier | Reels plays far below followers with high likes |
| IG feed | by followers: nano ~3–6%, micro ~1.5–4%, mid ~1–2.5%, macro ~0.8–1.5%, mega ~0.5–1.2% | — | Likes high, comments near zero |
| YouTube | (likes + comments) ÷ views ~3–6% | median views of videos 30–90 days old ~10–30% of subscribers (don't mix in videos a few days or years old) | under ~3% of subscribers = stale subscriber base |
| X | likes ÷ views ~0.5–2% | — | replies with no substance |
| LinkedIn | comments ~5–10% of reactions is normal; judge pods by **who** comments, not the ratio | — | the same 10–30 people commenting "Great insights!" within the first hour on every post (an engagement pod) |
| Newsletters | not public — subscriber band from badge or media kit, likes/comments per post, a public "advertise" page | claimed opens ~40–60% plausible; above ~70% needs proof | no proof of size at all |
| Podcasts | chart rank, review count, views of the YouTube version | downloads cannot be verified — mark "creator-claimed, verify" | — |

Tiers by followers: nano <10k · micro 10–100k · mid 100–500k · macro 500k–1M · mega 1M+. B2B voices are judged on who engages, not size.

## 4. Read the comments (50–100 across ~5 posts)

Red flags: more than ~30% emoji-only or generic ("Nice 🔥", "Love this"); letter+digit handles with no profile picture; comments that do not refer to the content; comment language that does not match the creator's or the target market's; the same commenters on every post. Comment-to-like ratio ~1–5% is typical on Instagram; under ~0.5% there with high likes suggests bought likes (TikTok ratios run lower — don't apply this flag there); over ~10% suggests a giveaway or pod.

Green flags: real questions about the product category, people describing themselves ("my two senior dogs", "as a nurse"), purchase intent ("where do I get this?") above ~2–5% of comments, creators replying to comments.

## 5. Signs of bought followers or engagement (inferred)

- Followers far above what views support (200k followers, a uniform ~2k views per post).
- Engagement suspiciously uniform post to post — real accounts have a long tail.
- Likes high, shares and saves near zero (where visible).
- Old posts that out-engage new ones by a wide margin while followers kept growing.
- Where TikTok audience countries exist: concentration in countries that do not match the creator's language or content.

## 6. Sponsored vs organic

- Sponsored median views ÷ organic median views should be ≥ ~0.7. Under ~0.5 = the audience tunes out ads; price on the sponsored median.
- More than ~1 in 3 recent posts sponsored = ad fatigue flag.
- The creator appears in a brand's Meta partnership ads = evidence a brand paid to amplify them (they convert well enough to scale) — and a possible competitor conflict.
- YouTube: check suspected sponsors per recent video (experimental signal, treat as a lead, not proof).

## 7. Cadence and consistency

Posted within ~14 days. Roughly ≥2 a week on TikTok, ≥1 a week on IG, ≥2 a month on YouTube, ≥1 a week on LinkedIn. Gaps over 30 days are a flag. A creator found through one viral outlier (research-market's saved outliers in the Inspiration library lean this way) must show a consistent median, not one hit.

## 8. Audience fit (state as a confidence level with evidence)

- **Comments are the audience sample**: self-descriptions, languages, places, purchase questions.
- **The creator's own content**: audiences roughly mirror the creator's niche and situation as the creator describes it; topic share ≥ ~50% of the last 30 posts on the niche.
- **TikTok audience countries** where pulled — countries only, and a pricier lookup: reach and install roles only, at most the top five finalists, shown as its own line at Gate 2.
- **B2B**: commenter headlines (function, seniority, company type) are the best evidence there is. A "LinkedIn influencer" whose engagement comes from other creators is not reaching buyers.
- Limits: commenters skew younger and more engaged than viewers; viral posts reach non-followers. **Never infer ethnicity, age, gender, skin tone or health from photos or names** — not the creator's, not their audience's. Use only what people say about themselves, quoted and counted ("14 of 80 commenters describe acne-prone skin").
- Confidence: **high** (direct evidence: audience countries + on-persona comments), **medium** (consistent content + comment signals), **low** (inference from content only — say so).

Category-specific fit: beauty needs the skin or hair concern the creator **states** in their own content; pet needs evidence the creator really has the animal; food needs regional availability and allergen awareness; apps need the creator's audience on the right store/region.

## 9. Brand safety (≥ ~12 months, ~50–100 posts including captions)

- Search the name with "scam", "apology", "drama", "controversy" (web search through the proxy); read call-outs in comment sections; notice deleted-looking gaps. If no search was possible, the verdict says "safety: captions and comments only" — never "clean".
- Politics, hate, explicit content, promotion of crypto schemes, gambling, MLMs, unlicensed medical or financial advice.
- Claims the brand could never make, made by the creator in past sponsored posts for the category (a supplement creator who promised cures).
- Transcripts of a few recent videos when captions are thin.

## 10. Competitor conflicts

Competitor partnership ads live in the Meta ad library; competitors' recent posts tagging the creator (the brand's own competitor organic feed is free); the creator's own posts tagging competitors in the last ~3–6 months. A recent competitor deal is either a conflict (possible exclusivity — the human asks) or evidence of category fit; say which and why. **Working for a rival or adjacent tool** (employee, founder, evangelist, ambassador — check the bio, LinkedIn headline and pinned post) is not a deal to ask about: it's a near-miss, with that reason.

## 11. Regulated categories and minors

- **Supplements, skincare, wellness**: signed-off structure/function or cosmetic claims only; no disease words ("cures", "treats", "heals", "pain relief"). **Acne**: "clears / treats / prevents acne or breakouts" are drug claims in the US (OTC acne products) — only if signed off in exactly those words. No unsubstantiated before/after.
- **Financial**: no promised returns, no "guaranteed"; crypto is high-risk; finance creators are regulated in several markets.
- **Alcohol**: creator 21+ (or local legal age) and an audience overwhelmingly of legal drinking age under industry codes; if it cannot be evidenced, the creator is out.
- **Minors**: a creator under 18 is out of scope for this playbook; kids regularly in content is a flag for regulated categories; "made for kids" YouTube channels are out.
- **Medical / pharmaceutical**: out unless the human confirms a compliance process exists.

## 13. UGC-for-ads rubric

The content runs on the brand's own ad account (or through partnership/Spark access), so the creator's audience barely matters. Judge:
- **On-camera delivery** — natural, specific, believable; holds attention in the first 2–3 seconds. The MCP cannot show you a creator's video, so the verdict says **"delivery not watched"**; judge what you can from transcripts (first lines, pacing of the words), ad-library reuse and their portfolio, and put 2–3 video links per UGC finalist in the roster for the human to watch before approving names.
- **Hook craft** — the first line of their best videos; variety of openings across posts.
- **Product handling and demo** — shows the thing clearly; good light and sound on a phone.
- **Past brand-ad work** — their content in brands' partnership ads (ad library) or a portfolio on their link-in-bio; did brands run it as ads (a sign it performed).
- **Turnaround and reliability** — posting cadence; stated turnaround on their portfolio.
- **Honesty risk** — creators who present every product as a years-long favourite. For a new product or app, the creator must actually use it before filming.
Skip audience fit and audience countries for this role.

## 14. B2B branch (LinkedIn voices, newsletters, podcasts)

- **LinkedIn**: the post sample from the person's recent posts (post search is keyword-based and may not return a clean last-12–20; record the real sample size and lower confidence when it is thin); reactions and comments per post; **who** comments — commenter headlines (function, seniority, company type) when the data exposes them; if it doesn't, say "commenter mix not checked" and lower the confidence. Pods are judged by repeat commenters, not ratios.
- **Newsletters**: size is creator-claimed unless a public badge or media kit shows it — mark "size creator-claimed — request the media kit"; sponsor history from past issues; topic fit.
- **Podcasts**: chart presence, review count, views of the YouTube version, past sponsors (spoken reads in transcripts).
- Fit is buyers, not reach: a 6k-follower LinkedIn voice whose commenters are finance leaders beats a 60k voice whose commenters are other creators.

## 15. What public pages show (the free pass)

Read in the host browser as a person would — a profile, a dozen posts, a few comment threads per creator; no scripted paging. What's visible logged-out changes often; check, and list what was hidden.

| Platform | Usually visible logged-out | Often hidden → paid top-up |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok | Followers, total likes, **views on every video in the profile grid**, likes / comments / shares / saves on a video page, many comments | Full comment threads, transcripts, audience countries |
| YouTube | Subscribers (rounded), views and upload date on every video, likes, comments | Transcripts at volume, suspected sponsors at volume |
| Instagram | Followers, post count, bio and link-in-bio, captions, sometimes reel plays and likes | Reel plays and likes on many accounts, most comments, tagged posts |
| LinkedIn | Headline, some recent posts with reaction and comment counts | Commenter headlines at volume, full post history |
| Newsletters, podcasts | Advertise / sponsor pages, Substack badges, chart positions, YouTube versions | Subscriber and download numbers (creator-claimed; ask for the media kit) |

The free verdict uses what was visible, labelled "public-page read <date>"; a creator whose key number is hidden is still shortlisted with that gap named, and the top-up fills it.

**No browser, only a fetch tool** (it often fails on TikTok and gets followers-only from Instagram): use, in this order, public creator-stats pages found by search (label "third-party stats, data date <date>" — they lag and round), the numbers in search-result snippets for a specific post (label them), YouTube pages (they fetch well), and the creator's own media kit. Never call a top-5 or a single post the median; say "typical views not read". Say in the report that the free read was fetch-only.

**Check every handle before listing it**: open the profile and match the name, niche and location to the source that named it — roundups and search snippets often give a wrong or stale handle. A mismatch is dropped, not shortlisted.
