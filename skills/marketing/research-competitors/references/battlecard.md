# Human version

A battlecard is the short sheet a salesperson can use before a competitive call. It turns the rival research into fair comparisons, useful questions and answers to real objections, with proof beside each claim.

---

# Agent version

## Build the card

1. **Frame the deal.** Use the named rival, product, buyer group and deal situation from the ask/brain. With no deal context, use the saved lead buyer, label that assumption and make confirming it a closing decision. Read the rival record, our product facts and approved claims, the audience report/objection evidence and any owner-supplied win/loss notes. Public complaints are objection hypotheses; only actual deal evidence establishes win/loss patterns.
2. **Close the comparison gaps.** Recheck the rival's current product/pricing pages for the capabilities and typical-buyer price used on the card. Use the same unit and use case on both sides; show required tiers and hidden costs. Include a strength of theirs and a situation in which they fit better. Unknown capability means unknown, not absent. Use the existing rival-pass for missing dimensions only (`../../research-competitors-rival-pass/SKILL.md`); do not run a whole category study.
3. **Write usable answers.** For each evidenced objection: buyer's words/source → acknowledge the trade-off → our supported difference → proof or a fair evaluation question. Questions ask the buyer to test a real requirement; they never presume a rival cannot meet it. Draft two short email/chat replies for common competitive moments. Reuse the brand voice and approved offers; do not invent a switch discount, customer result or migration service.
4. **Claims check.** Apply the `claims-gate` skill (`../../claims-gate/SKILL.md`) to our promises and every comparative sales line. Named comparisons also need the marketer's sign-off and dated like-for-like evidence. Show unapproved lines as clearly marked draft proposals, outside the copy-ready answers; give a safe product-fact answer where possible. Ask for the smallest needed sign-off at the end. A customer's approval is not independent proof of a rival's limitation.

## Output

Follow write-report's summary, decisions and final checks. The usable sales sheet should fit about one page (roughly 600–900 words before the evidence appendix):

- **In short / 30-second reference:** who each product fits; where we win and lose; the best opening question; how sure and the key evidence limit.
- **Decisions for you:** deal assumption and comparative lines needing approval, only when present.
- **Deal fit:** win / lose / close situations, with evidence or explicitly labelled hypothesis. No fabricated win-rate.
- **Evaluation questions:** 3–5 questions tied to the buyer's real requirements; what each answer would change.
- **Objections and responses:** at least three distinct, sourced objections, or every supported one with “evidence is thin” said plainly. Provide finished responses, not directions to write responses.
- **Feature and typical-buyer cost comparison:** our and their status, date/source, honest unknowns and trade-offs.
- **Two short replies:** “we use them already” and “why choose you?”; adapt when the evidence supports different moments.
- **What to do / What we're not doing / What we couldn't check / Sources:** keep these brief. The appendix maps each comparison to dated evidence and approval status.

## Completion check

A salesperson can use the opening question and at least one safe finished response without doing the research again. Every brand/comparative promise has a proof and approval status; current pricing has one scenario and units; where the rival wins is visible. Any unknown is honest, not a disguised weakness. Missing proof is named in Decisions, and the card is labelled draft if the copy-ready comparison still needs sign-off. Save/deliver through focused-outputs.md.
