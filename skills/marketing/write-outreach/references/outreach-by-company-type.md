# Outreach by company type

Load at step 1. Who is being pitched changes the channel, the ask and the tone. Pick the row matching the list's motion (research-audience's fit definition says which).

| Seller → buyer | Channel that works | The ask | What to say | Watch-outs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **B2B SaaS → buying committee** | Email + LinkedIn | 15-minute call, or a resource | Their signal, the job they're hired to do, one plain product fact | Different angle per role (champion: daily pain; budget owner: cost and risk); never the same text to two people at one account |
| **Software or services sold to marketers** (agencies, martech) | Email + LinkedIn | A sample made for them, a short call | What their public ads or content show (linked), as a question | Never criticise their creative; a sample is a strong ask only if the marketer will make it |
| **Agency or services firm → new clients** | Email + LinkedIn, referrals | A short call about a specific problem | A concrete observation about their business and one relevant past result (signed off) | Spec work on request only; no "we're a full-service agency" |
| **DTC / consumer brand → retail buyers and shops** | Chains: the vendor portal or buying office first, then the category buyer; independents: the shop's published inbox, a visit, or (UK sole traders) a letter | Samples, a line sheet, a slot at the next review | Why it sells on their shelf (price tier, who buys it, what it sits next to), the category review window; margin and minimums only from the brand's wholesale sheet — else "wholesale terms needed" as a decision | Buyers work to category calendars — say the window; never cold-DM a UK sole trader |
| **CPG → distributors and brokers** | Email + phone (where allowed) + trade-show meetings | An intro call, samples | Velocity elsewhere (signed off), the territory, margin structure | Channel conflict: never pitch a territory with an existing distributor |
| **Industrial / B2B non-SaaS → purchasing** | Email, phone where allowed, trade shows | A quote request, a site visit | Specs, lead times, certifications | Many buyers aren't on LinkedIn; formal tone |
| **Marketplace → supply side** (providers, sellers) | Email, IG or the business's listed channel | Sign up, a short call | Demand they'd get (only real numbers), how easy listing is | Larger lists; keep messages short and transactional |
| **Consumer app → partners** | Email + LinkedIn to BD or partnerships | An intro call | The shared audience and a specific joint idea | Small, hand-picked lists; one partner, one idea |
| **Developer tools → technical buyers** | Email to the buyer; GitHub-aware angle | Try it, a short technical call | The use case their team's public work shows | Never message developers from commit data; write to the buyer, credit the team's work in general terms |
| **Nonprofit → corporate partners, foundations** | Email to CSR or programme officers | An intro call around a grant cycle | Cause fit, the partner's own stated goals | Corporate and foundation contacts only |

## Tone by seniority

Founders and managers: peer to peer, short. Directors and VPs: crisp, outcome-first, fewer words. C-suite at large companies: rarely cold — write to the director who owns the problem (research-audience picks the person by company size).
