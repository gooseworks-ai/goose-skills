# Cold email craft

Load at steps 1–4 and in outreach-qa. A cold email is read in a few seconds on a phone by someone who owes the sender nothing. It earns a reply by being about **their** situation, short, true, and easy to answer.

Numbers here are rules of thumb from outbound practice, not measured for this brand; the brand's own results (outcomes-ledger lessons) beat them as soon as they exist.

## The shape of touch 1

1. **First line — the hook.** Their signal or a fact about their work, and why it matters to the pain. Never about the sender. ("Saw Brightfold has two AP specialist roles open — usually a sign month-end close is slipping.")
2. **One or two lines — evidence.** The pain in the buyers' own words, and what we do about it. Proof only if signed off (claims-gate); otherwise a plain product fact or nothing.
3. **One line — the ask.** One low-friction question.
4. **Sign-off:** first name, or name and role. No "Best regards", no title block, no image signature.

**Length:** touch 1 is 50–90 words; follow-ups 30–50; the last touch 20–30; a warm reconnection 40–70. Two or three short paragraphs; four is the cap. Over 120 words fails QA.

## Frameworks — pick by the situation

| Framework | Shape | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **Signal → proof → ask** | Their signal, what we do about it, a soft ask | Any signal-led touch (the default) |
| **Problem → agitate → solve** | The pain, what it costs them, the fix | A stated pain or a need implied by news |
| **Before → after → bridge** | Where they are, where they want to be, how | A growing team, a new hire, a new leader's agenda |
| **Attention → interest → desire → action** | A sharp opener, relevance, the outcome, the ask | A cold list with no signal (weakest — prefer a segment angle) |
| **Relationship → context → ask** | How you know them, why now, a light ask | Warm only: past champions, past users, win-back |

## Personalisation — how deep

| Depth | What changes per person | When |
|---|---|---|
| Segment | Pain and proof per segment; merge fields per row | Large lists with no per-person signal |
| Signal | A first line from each person's dated signal or hook | Lists from research-audience (the default) |
| Deep | Signal plus something they said or wrote | Top-value accounts, a handful a day |

A hook is useful only if it ties to the pain. "Loved your post on brand storytelling" proves research happened and nothing else. Strongest hooks, in order: something they wrote or said recently (≤60 days); their own job post; their recent move; a concrete event at their company. **Never:** school, family, health, religion, politics, location, personal accounts, "#OpenToWork", or a fact you can't link.

**Never lie about how you found them.** No "came across your profile" for a list built from search; no "we met at…" without a meeting; no fake "Re:" or "Fwd:". (Warm contacts flip this rule: name exactly how you know each other.)

## Subject lines

Under 50 characters (QA flags over 60); lower-case or sentence case; no "!", emoji or all-caps; no "quick question", "touching base", "free", "limited time". Shapes that work:
- Their signal: "before you fill the ap roles"
- Their pain as a question: "still matching invoices by hand?"
- The topic for them: "month-end close at brightfold"
- A peer angle (only with a signed-off peer example): "how <peer group> handle <pain>"
- A switch (only when they said it): "looking for an <rival> alternative?"

Follow-ups reply in the same thread (no new subject) unless the angle changes completely.

## The ask

One per message, smallest that moves things: "worth a look?", "open to a 15-minute call next week?", "want the one-page breakdown?", "who owns creative there?" Never two asks; never a calendar link in touch 1 to a stranger; never "let me know your thoughts". Later touches can build: an overview → a short walkthrough → a resource with no call at all.

## Hard rules

1. No filler opener ("hope this finds you well", "my name is", "I'm reaching out because").
2. No "just checking in", "bumping this", "circling back". Every follow-up adds something new or isn't sent.
3. Nothing in the first sentence about us.
4. One ask.
5. No invented facts, numbers, customers, results or compliments. A number needs a signed-off claim behind it.
6. No jargon: synergy, leverage, circle back, touch base, align, ping, revolutionary, game-changing, cutting-edge, best-in-class.
7. No attachments in touch 1; at most one link, and none in touch 1 when the mailbox is new.
8. Plain text, no images, no tracking pixels (open tracking hurts delivery and is unreliable — Apple Mail pre-loads images; judge on replies).
9. A real person signs and can answer.
10. The opt-out line and the sender's identity on every email (handoff-and-compliance.md).

## Tone

Peer to peer for founders and managers ("casual-direct"); crisp and brief for directors and above; empathetic when the hook is a pain; never needy. A sender's voice card, when one exists, sets the words; these rules still apply.

## Before / after (fictional seller of accounts-payable software — never reuse its values)

Weak: "Hi Jordan, I hope you're well! I'm reaching out from Ledgerly, the AI-powered platform revolutionising finance teams. We help companies like yours cut costs by 80%. Do you have 30 minutes this week for a demo?"
- About us, an invented number, a hype word, a big ask, no hook.

Better: "Hi Jordan — saw Brightfold has two AP specialist roles open since the 2nd. Teams usually add AP headcount when month-end close starts slipping. We match invoices to POs automatically, so new hires start on exceptions, not data entry. Worth a look before they start? — Robin"
- Hook from her signal, the pain, a plain product fact, one small ask, 50 words.
