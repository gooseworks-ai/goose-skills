# Idea validation — "is there demand for this?"

Load when the ask names a product, offer, feature or concept that doesn't exist yet ("would people buy a …?", "is there demand for …?", "should we launch …?"). It's the same market research, pointed at one idea: the report keeps write-report's shape, and its first line is the verdict. Directional evidence, never proof of product-market fit and never a replacement for the brand's own sales data.

## 1. Write the hypotheses first

Three to five, each with what would support it and what would weaken it. Typical ones: the problem is frequent · it is urgent · today's alternatives fall short · people would pay roughly <price> · the message "<promise>" lands with <buyer>. Write them before reading anything, so the evidence can't be bent to fit.

## 2. Collect the evidence

From the same free pass as a market report, aimed at the hypotheses: what buyers say about the problem (reviews of today's alternatives, threads, comments, video transcripts), what they use instead (direct rivals, substitutes, workarounds, doing nothing), rivals' offers and prices, search phrasings and their direction. When the category sells through social commerce, add a pass on TikTok Shop and Amazon: listings, prices, offers, ratings, review themes, which creators already sell nearby products. A shop listing or a creator showcase is not proof of sales.

## 3. Separate attention from buying

Views, likes and saves are **attention**. Questions about price or availability, comparisons, "I bought it", repeat use and recommendations are **buying signals** (the buying scale in `listening-craft.md` § Classify). Product requests and workarounds are the strongest signs of an unmet need. Count each kind separately.

## 4. Score each hypothesis

On five things, in words: how strong the evidence is · how consistent it is across sources · how recent · how varied the sources are · how close it is to an actual purchase. A hypothesis resting on one source, or only on attention, is "weak".

## 5. Recommend the cheapest next test

One test with a pass mark, chosen to settle the weakest important hypothesis cheaply: five buyer interviews · a landing page with a waitlist (pass: <n> sign-ups from <n> visitors) · a pre-order or deposit · an offer test to existing customers · one creator test · an ad-message test (pass: <click or sign-up rate> against the brand's usual).

## The report's top

- **In short** (write-report §1): "What we looked at" (the idea, the sources, the date, the main limit); then "**Verdict:** proceed · refine · test further · weak evidence", with the 2–3 findings behind it, each with how sure; then "So, do this first (because of …)": the test and its pass mark.
- **Decisions for you:** go or no-go on the test, the price to test, who runs it.
- In the story, a hypothesis table: hypothesis · what supports it (linked) · what weakens it (linked) · score in words. No market-size claims beyond what a source supports.
