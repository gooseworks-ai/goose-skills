# Business types: what to infer first, what's usually worth asking

**Summary.** For each business type, a few facts that tend to pass the asking rule, why a wrong
guess costs money, and where to look before asking. These are examples, not a checklist.
Whatever is not listed, work out from the rule in `SKILL.md`.

Always try the "infer it first" column before asking. A stated assumption the user can correct
costs them a glance; a question costs them a turn.

## Software

| Fact | Why a wrong guess costs money | Infer it first from |
|---|---|---|
| Self-serve sign-up or a sales call | Decides what the ad asks for: a trial or a demo. The wrong one sends people to a dead end | The pricing page ("Start free" vs "Book a demo"), the site's main button |
| Typical yearly deal size | Decides what a lead or trial is worth, and so how much each can cost | Public pricing tiers × likely seats. No public pricing → ask, as ranges |
| Time from first look to paying | Decides how long a test must run before it can show paying customers | Trial length on the pricing page; "contact sales" usually means weeks to months |
| Who signs off on buying | Decides who the ads speak to | Case studies, the "for teams / for enterprises" pages |

## E-commerce

| Fact | Why a wrong guess costs money | Infer it first from |
|---|---|---|
| Typical order value | Sets the most a sale can cost before it loses money | Product prices in the catalog, bundles, free-shipping threshold |
| Share of visitors who buy | Tells you whether the site or the ads are the bottleneck | Rarely public → ask as ranges, or "unknown — ask at strategy" |
| Abandoned carts | A high rate means reminder ads to people who nearly bought may pay back first | Rarely public → ask only if the user seems to know their store numbers |
| How often people reorder | Consumables with repeat orders can afford a higher first-order cost | Subscriptions or "subscribe & save" on the site; the product type |
| Margin | Turns order value into what a sale is really worth | Never public → ask when the budget is large enough for it to matter |

## App

| Fact | Why a wrong guess costs money | Infer it first from |
|---|---|---|
| Share of installs that start paying | Cheap installs that never pay are wasted money | Never public → ask as ranges |
| Revenue per paying user, and the main plan | Sets what a paying user is worth | App Store in-app purchase list; the most prominent plan is only a guess — state it |
| Trial length | Decides when a test can see payments | The store listing, the paywall screenshots |
| Store only, or also web sign-up | Decides where people land | The website's main button |

## Every business type

| Fact | Why it matters | Infer it first from |
|---|---|---|
| What ads may claim (named customers, results, "clinically proven", "#1") | Claims and legal exposure | `brand.md` if already recorded; otherwise **always ask**. Default: none |
| Regulated category rules | Legal exposure; Meta restricts targeting for some | The product itself. If it's health, finance, housing, jobs, alcohol, dating, weight loss or politics, ask what must never be said or targeted |
| Most they'll spend per day, ever | Money; a hard safety limit for later stages | Ask with the budget question if it isn't obvious from the total |
| Familiarity with Meta ads | Sets the tone of every later message | The user's own wording; otherwise one question |
