# Podcast and street-interview dialogue

**Summary.** A conversation needs two people responding to each other. Product facts
authorize claims; observed conversations teach turn structure. Write the exchange
first, then fit timing and visuals. A feature list split between speakers is a failed
conversation even when every fact is correct.

## Gather the right reference

Reuse one or two inspected same-format sources from the angle research or project.
Prefer the brand's existing work, then relevant category creative. Read or transcribe
a complete exchange, not just its hook or caption. Record who initiates, what the
other person actually answers, what prompts the follow-up and how the exchange ends.
Use short quotations only when needed; preserve source links and paraphrase mechanics.

Do not invent an observation. A publisher transcript supports words and turn order;
it does not establish vocal delivery or camera reactions. A real editorial interview
can be a provisional conversation reference, but its authority, claims, reputation and
performance do not transfer to an advertisement. Search for missing commercial
references when useful; if access fails, keep that limitation explicit.

## Write an exchange, then an ad

- Define what each person wants from this moment. Give the second person a role beyond
  prompting the sales pitch.
- Start with a concrete question, observed action or disagreement. Let the answer change
  the follow-up. Use the reply's detail instead of moving down a prepared feature list.
- Vary turn length where it serves the exchange. A clarification can be two words;
  an explanation may need more. Do not add filler, fake laughter or interruptions as
  decoration.
- Keep the product explanation selective. Demonstrate one useful detail. Put supporting
  specifications, website and required copy in an insert or ending where appropriate.
- Preserve a real speaker's testimony only with attribution and authorization. Staged
  dialogue can discuss a problem or present an observable action, not invent purchase
  history, a professional credential or a product result.

## Distinguish the formats

**Podcast:** the hosts may know the topic but differ in what they assume or care about.
A skeptical follow-up or correction should change the explanation. A clip can start
mid-discussion if the opening is still intelligible. Do not simulate a named host,
expert endorsement or genuine unsponsored episode.

**Street interview:** ask something an unprepared person could answer. A response may
be mundane, practical or unexpected. Follow that answer. When an actor demonstrates a
product, keep the reaction about what is observable, not a made-up review.
Disclose a staged ad; do not pretend it is consumer research.

## Review without the scene directions

Read only the words as an alternating exchange. For each turn ask:

1. Why would this person say it now?
2. What in the previous turn caused it?
3. Would someone use this wording out loud?
4. Is it explaining a useful detail or volunteering a marketing slogan?

Reject convenient setup questions, unprompted spec recitals, identical sentence rhythm,
rehearsed admiration and slogan-shaped answers. For example, “What would you change
first?” followed by a catalogue sentence gives no evidence of actual listening.
Contractions alone do not make it human.

Treat 4/5 as usable with minor delivery edits and 5/5 as ready to perform as written.
Natural language and conversational logic must each clear the bar; their average must
not hide a weak exchange. A model score still needs calibration against operator
rejections and accepted examples. Never relabel an earlier metric to manufacture a pass.
