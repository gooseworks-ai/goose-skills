# An excellent script — worked example

*Its clipped rhythm is one style; a brand voice learning (e.g. "spoken lines flow as natural sentences") wins over it. Its "3:07am" scenes show a person in bed: if that person is generated, the hook is a labelled dramatisation and the person never speaks about using the product (speaker test).*

*Hypothetical brand "Hushwell Sleep Gummies", US market, Meta Reels + TikTok, 9:16, 30s. Every input below stands in for what the brand brain returns; nothing here may be reused for a real brand.*

**On file.** Approved claims / facts: C1 "Supports restful sleep." · C2 fact: 3 mg melatonin + 200 mg L-theanine per gummy · C3 "Non-habit-forming." · C4 label directions: "Take one gummy 30 minutes before bed." · C5 signed claim (Brand record): "4.7 stars from 12,000+ reviews" · C6 offer: "30-night money-back guarantee".
Customer evidence: V1 "my brain does a full replay of every awkward thing I've ever said, at 3am" · V2 "I was scared of getting hooked on sleep pills" · V3 "tastes like actual berries, not chalk" · V4 "I don't feel like a zombie the next morning" (a benefit not on the approved list → **cut**).
Angle bank: A3 "the 3am replay" — racing-mind sleeplessness, a recurring review theme competitors are not using (problem_fix, from customer_words).

## The lead script — "3am Replay"

One message: for the racing 3am brain, a gentle, non-habit-forming gummy. ~56 spoken words.

| Time | Picture | On-screen | Voice / sound | Trace |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2 HOOK | Extreme close-up: eyes open in a dark room, lit by a phone showing **3:07**. The jar soft-focus on the nightstand from frame 1 | **3:07am.** My brain: "remember what you said in 2014?" | No voice. A clock tick, one phone buzz | V1 / A3. No claim |
| 2–6 AGITATE | Three hard cuts: ceiling, pillow flip, phone at 3:22, then 3:41 | the replay → 3:22 → 3:41 | "The 3 a.m. replay. Every awkward thing… in HD." | V1. Small "Dramatization" if a generated person appears |
| 6–10 PRODUCT | A hand opens the jar — **real product image**, label front-on. One gummy into the palm | **Hushwell Sleep** · 3 mg melatonin + L-theanine | "So we made Hushwell Sleep. Three milligrams of melatonin, plus L-theanine." | C2. The brand speaks, not a customer |
| 10–15 USE + BENEFIT | Gummy eaten; phone face-down; lamp clicks off (no "instantly asleep" cut) | One gummy, 30 min before bed · **Supports restful sleep** | "One gummy, thirty minutes before bed, to support restful sleep." | C4, C1 exact |
| 15–21 OBJECTION | Review-style card, then a gummy macro | "I was scared of getting hooked on sleep pills." — Verified buyer → **Non-habit-forming** | "Non-habit-forming. And it tastes like berries — not chalk." | V2 as the customer's *fear*; C3 exact; V3 taste opinion. No "candy"/"treat" |
| 21–26 PROOF | Real review screenshots scrolling, then the stars | **4.7★ from 12,000+ reviews** | "Twelve thousand reviews. Four point seven stars." | C5 as written |
| 26–30 CTA | Jar on the nightstand in morning light — **no person** (avoids "wake refreshed") — end card | **Try 30 nights. Love it or your money back.** + disclaimer per brand rules, held ≥ 3s | "Try it for thirty nights — love it or your money back." | C6 |

Cut at the gate: V4 "not a zombie next morning" — implies a no-grogginess benefit not on the approved list; it goes to the claims gate's scoped sign-off at the gate ("Can the ad say people don't feel groggy the next morning?"), with its proof; declined → cut.

## Why it is excellent — vs the junior version

| Excellent | Junior |
|---|---|
| Frame 1 works as a still: a time, a funny line, the product in shot | Logo animation and "Introducing Hushwell!" |
| The hook is one recognisable moment from real reviews | "Struggling with insomnia?" — a disease word plus a personal attribute: rejected |
| One message, one proof, one objection | Five benefits: faster, deeper, refreshed, less anxiety, immunity |
| Benefit words are the approved words, untouched | "Knocks you out in 15 minutes", "clinically proven" |
| Customers speak only through real quotes; the brand speaks as the brand | A generated woman says "these changed my life" — a fake testimonial |
| ~56 words in 30s; cuts every 1.3–4s; 4–9 words per card | 95 words, sped-up voice, walls of text |
| Product cue at 0s, named at 6s, back at the CTA | Product first appears at 24s |
| Reads perfectly muted | Meaning lost with sound off |
