# Intake craft

Load at steps 2 and 3. How a strategist fills the gaps in week one without turning it into a form.

## Scope: what is settled today and what waits

The founder answers a small set of brand-owned facts now. Everything else is recorded as unknown, with the playbook that will ask for it at the moment it needs it. Page 01's rule: ask about ten facts on day one, never a long form.

**Settled in this playbook (confirm a draft, never ask cold):**

| Day-one fact | What "settled" means |
|---|---|
| What we sell, and the hero offer | The products the brain found are right; which one is the hero; the standing offer, if any; anything sold out or never to advertise (`ad_eligibility: hidden`, with the founder's reason). |
| Who it is for | The founder's own one-line answer, confirmed against the brain's audience read; who it is *not* for. Top objections are not asked here: they come from research-market's customer evidence (ledger: "waits for customer research"). |
| Company type and stage | The brain's `brandType` confirmed; stage in the founder's words (pre-launch, launched, growing, stalled). |
| What we may claim | The claims audit and sign-off (claims-craft.md). |
| What we must never say or do | The never-say list: law, platform and owner entries (settled in step 4, with the one question about what must not be said publicly). |
| Voice examples, logo and colours | Voice sign-off (voice-craft.md); logo, colours and fonts confirmed (step 6). |
| Who approves | Who signs off claims and voice. If it is not the person in the chat, new lines wait for the approver on the sign-off sheet; claims already approved stay approved. |
| One-line positioning, if the founder has one | Their own words: for whom, what it is, instead of what. If they have none, record it as unknown and name write-my-positioning (backlog); do not write positioning here. |
| What buyers use instead of us | One question: who they lose to, or (pre-revenue) what buyers would use instead, including doing nothing. Names only; track the ones they name; research-competitors does the rest. |

**Recorded as unknown, asked later by the playbook that needs it:**

| Fact | Asked by (internal; in the record write "when you …") |
|---|---|
| The goal number, deadline and budget | where-to-start (situation questions) or plan-campaign |
| Margins, most we can pay per customer | plan-campaign, run-paid-campaigns, email-lifecycle |
| What the agent may do alone, spend caps | run-paid-campaigns |
| This week's focus, next big date, live promotions | plan-campaign, email-lifecycle |
| Account access | whichever playbook needs the account |
| Search terms | improve-search-visibility |
| How results are counted | analyze-performance |
| Creators, partners, press relationships | work-with-creators |
| Story, origin and beliefs | make-content |

If the founder volunteers any of these, record them with provenance. Never refuse a fact; just don't ask for it.

## How to ask

- **Read first.** Documents the founder hands over are imported before any question (step 2). Never ask anything the site, the brain or an uploaded document answers.
- **Draft, then confirm.** "I have your customer as: women 25–40 with adult acne who've tried prescriptions and hated the dryness. Right, or closer to something else?" Never ask an open question when a draft is possible.
- **Group, don't interrogate.** One message per group (what we sell; who it's for; claims by risk; never-say; voice). Each item has a suggested answer, why it matters in a few words, and an easy "don't know". The basics take about 5–8 decisions and under 10 minutes; the whole run about 15–20 decisions and 20–30 minutes (SKILL.md time budget).
- **No jargon** the founder didn't use first: no ICP, CAC, LTV, USP.
- **Never ask:** for three adjectives; "what's your USP / target audience / brand personality?" as open questions; rating scales; passwords; anything already answered this session.
- **"Don't know" is an answer.** Record it as unknown with the assumption in use. It is never re-asked next session unless something changed.

## Contradictions

Surface every contradiction you find; never pick a side silently.

- **Precedence when the owner is unavailable:** what the owner says > the owner's documents > rules the owner made earlier (in learnings) > the brand's own site > the server's research. Inside the research itself, look for clashes too: a price on the site vs in an ad, an old tagline still live, a product the catalogue lists that the site no longer sells.
- **Show both sides** with source and date, the interim default, and the question: "Site footer (read 14 Oct): free shipping over $45. Your product page banner: over $35. Which is current? Until you say, nothing will mention a threshold."
- **Saved sources.** `brand_read` `sections: ["sources"]` lists the social accounts and review profiles research found, with `confirmed: false`. Show the brand's own ones (relationship `self`) and ask the owner to confirm they are really theirs. A wrong handle poisons the voice read and any later review import.
- Log each resolution in the brand record: who decided, when, in their words.

## Provenance for every fact

Every fact in the brand record carries:
- **Source:** told by owner / told by agency / approver via agency / owner's document / found on site / server research / agent note (unconfirmed) / inferred.
- **Date** captured, and **confirmed by** whom and when.
- **Visibility:** public / internal only / embargoed until a date / under NDA. Revenue, margin, budget and customer names default to internal.
- **Scope:** whole brand / one product / one audience / one market or location.

## The unknowns ledger

One line per gap:
- the gap (and its knowledge section);
- the job it blocks, and whether it blocks or only degrades that job;
- the assumption in use meanwhile, labelled **guess**;
- who was asked and when (or "not asked yet"), and when it will be asked, in plain words ("when you plan a campaign"): the owner reads the ledger, so no playbook names in it;
- what reopens it.

## Company-type add-ons

Ask these only when they apply **and** change a claim, an offer or a rule now; record the rest as unknown. Each is still draft-then-confirm.

- **DTC / e-commerce:** returns, shipping thresholds and zones, guarantee; standing discount rules and the deepest discount allowed; subscription, loyalty and referral programmes with their rewards and tiers (so no email promises the wrong reward); hero SKUs and never-advertise SKUs.
- **B2B SaaS:** which customers may be named (default none; written permission per name, with who granted it and when; design partners are a material connection) and which case studies are cleared; security and compliance certificates as expiring claims; competitor-naming policy; public pricing vs "talk to sales"; plan and tier names; roadmap items that are embargoed; analyst-quote rules.
- **Consumer app:** store ratings per platform and country (as expiring claims); trial and pricing terms per platform; kids or health-data flags.
- **Marketplace seller:** which claims are allowed on each marketplace (stricter than the own site); listing copy pasted or uploaded by the owner (the agent cannot read Amazon pages); authorised sellers and minimum advertised price policy.
- **Local or multi-location:** per-location name, address, phone, hours, services and offers; who controls local marketing (franchise or head office). The brain has no per-location shape today: record each location in the brand record and say so.
- **Services firm:** service packages and what each includes; team credentials and bios; client-naming default none; professional advertising rules for the profession.
- **Regulated category** (beauty, supplements, food, finance, alcohol, kids, health care, weight loss, CBD): load the rule pack from category-rules.md before drafting any claim; claims default to product facts only.
- **Several markets or languages:** tag claims, prices and rules by market.
