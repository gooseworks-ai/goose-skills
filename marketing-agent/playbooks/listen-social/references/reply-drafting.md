# Reply drafting

Load at step 7. Every draft is finished — something the human could send as written — and carries a recommendation, not a question.

## Fields on every draft

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| id | R-NN for this week |
| item | the post or comment, quoted short, with link, date, platform |
| author context | customer? (as far as the post shows) · reach · history with the brand if known |
| route and recommendation | reply publicly / take private / stay silent — and why in one line |
| draft | the reply (and, for take private, the public line plus the private message) |
| claims used | each factual statement and its approved-claim source; "none" is fine |
| voice check | matches the confirmed voice rules / "voice not yet confirmed — neutral draft" (when the claims gate says so) |
| banned words | checked against the brand's list |
| disclosure | affiliation line required? (per `../../../atoms/ad-policy-check/disclosure.md` §1) |
| risk | what could go wrong; S1/S2 escalation note with the contact |
| founder voice? | if the reply is in the founder's name, flagged for the founder's own yes |

## Rules

- **Facts only as the claims gate allows** (`../../../atoms/claims-gate/ATOM.md`): the brand's approved "Claims you may use" lines once signed off; before that, plain product facts and published policies only. If a helpful answer needs a fact that is not approved, write around it or flag "needs a fact from the brand: <what>". Reply drafts are never sent through the gate's scoped sign-off on their own — ask only if a crisis reply truly needs the line.
- **Regulated categories** — skincare, supplements, pet health, health and mental health (ADHD and other conditions are health), finance, kids, alcohol, employment and AI in hiring, privacy and call-recording consent: never diagnose, never suggest a cause, never give medical, legal or financial advice, never promise results, never assert compliance unless it is an approved claim. Pausing use and contacting support or a professional is the safe default.
- **Disclosure** follows the shared [`disclosure.md`](../../../atoms/ad-policy-check/disclosure.md) §1: someone employed by or paid by the brand discloses the connection in the reply itself. On **third-party communities** (Reddit, forums, Facebook groups, Discord) that means the draft opens with it ("I work at <brand>"). Check the community's rules on brand or self-promotional replies (read the rules page with the host's browser or fetch); if they ban them, or you could not check, recommend stay silent and say "rules unchecked". Never draft a post that poses as a customer, and never suggest sock-puppet accounts.
- **Competitor complaints:** answer the person's need with approved facts; never name, mock or knock the rival.
- **Refunds and compensation:** only what the policy in the brain says; otherwise "our team will look after you" and take it private.
- **Holding statements** follow `crisis-triage.md`: brand-wide only when 3+ separate people raise it, press picks it up, or contamination, a recall or legal action is confirmed by the brand, a regulator or the press; any single-poster item gets an on-post reply and a private draft, the statement held with its trigger.
- **Crisis hold:** while an S1 or S2 about a product is open, sales-signal, poaching and promo replies touching that product default to stay silent.
- **Pre-launch brands:** poaching replies and sales-signal replies default to stay silent; never invent a waitlist offer or perk.
- **Short beats long** on social. Use the poster's name when public. Sign with a first name if the brand does.
- **Stay silent is a real recommendation** — for trolls, bait, a thread where any brand reply would amplify, or communities that ban brands. Say why.

## Example drafts (fictional dog-food brand — shape only, never copy)

**A. A dog got sick after switching (S2, one high-reach poster) — recommend: reply on her post, then take private; route to support and the adverse-event log.**
> "Hi [name], we're so sorry — that sounds really worrying, and we want to help. Please check in with your vet, and send us a DM with your order email so our team can look into it with you personally. — [first name], [Brand]"

Claims used: none. Risk: no explanation of why it happened, no "transition period" line, no refund promise unless the policy says so. Wider statement: not recommended — one poster; a brand-wide post would amplify. Escalation: S2 → [contact].

**B. Holding statement for a contamination claim several people are repeating (S1, unverified) — recommend: post once the human sets the time; escalate now.**
> "We've seen the posts about [issue] and we're looking into it right now. We'll share what we find here by [time — set by you]. If your dog has been affected, please contact [support channel] so we can help directly."

Must not: deny, blame, speculate on cause, use legal language, or mention batch numbers before the facts are checked. Wake: [escalation contact]. The human sends it.

**C. Reddit thread asking for a recommendation, complaining about a competitor — recommend: reply only if r/[sub] allows affiliated replies (rules checked); otherwise stay silent.**
> "Disclosure: I work at [Brand]. If switching foods has been rough on his stomach, ours is [approved fact]. Happy to answer anything — and no pressure either way."

Risk: never name or knock the competitor; approved facts only.

**D. Happy customer tagging the brand on Instagram — recommend: reply publicly; UGC rights request.**
> "This made our day, [name] — thank you for sharing! Would you be open to us resharing this? We'll credit you, of course."

Note: asking is fine; reuse waits for a written yes (the human handles the rights).
