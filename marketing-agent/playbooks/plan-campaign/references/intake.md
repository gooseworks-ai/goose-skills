# Intake — look first, then ask like a person

Load in Before you start. The first hand test failed here: the agent asked a seven-part form (funnel rates, churn, margin, tracking, hours…) before checking what GooseWorks already knew, never asked which product or offer, and asked a technical question the founder couldn't answer ("Does the signup event fire, and are UTMs in place?"). A good marketer does the opposite: reads everything they can, then asks three or four plain questions.

## 1. Look first (free, before the first message)

| Look at | It answers | Don't ask about it if found |
|---|---|---|
| `brain_map` (when `brain_*` tools are listed) and its chapters 2, 3, 5, 15, 18, 19, 20, 23; else `brand_read` and `knowledge_search` | rules, products, what sells, who buys, approvals and budget, how results are counted, what's coming, targets and past tests, what is working | anything the brain already holds — items waiting for a yes are used, labelled as waiting |
| the last plan record and the run records since it (`references/run-memory.md`) | how last time's legs and tests did, money moves and their results | "how did last month go?" — say what the records show |
| `brand_read` products (with prices for the hero products) | what's for sale, plans and prices | which product exists, what it costs |
| `brand_read` onboarding | who makes the ads, who runs them, platforms used, spend band, stated goals | "who makes the ads?", "which platforms?" |
| `brand_read` learnings (full) + `knowledge_search` for the task | past results, offers that ran, rules | "what worked before?" |
| `meta_read` pages + performance | is Meta connected; recent spend and results on ads GooseWorks made | "do you run Meta ads?" |
| `social_account_list` | which organic accounts exist | "do you post on Instagram/TikTok?" |
| `campaign_read` + `ads_creative_read` (approvals view) | campaigns already planned, offers and audiences used, creative waiting | duplicates; "is this the Halloween one?" — say what you see instead |
| `lifecycle/program.md`, `lifecycle/calendar/<YYYY-MM>.md` | email/SMS list size, flows, sends already planned, standing offer | "do you have an email list?" |
| `reports/performance/definitions.md` + newest weekly read | margin, the real number (store / CRM), tracking state, recent results | margin, "is tracking set up?" |
| the last approved plan's money and status sections | AOV, margin, list size, founder hours given last time | anything already stated |
| research files (market read, personas, customer words, demand; competitor report, angle bank) | who buys, why, what rivals run | "who's your customer?" |
| **the brand's live site — required every run** (host web fetch / browser): home and pricing pages | live offer, prices, positioning; beats saved notes and old plans | "is there a sale on?" — confirm instead of asking |
| an existing plan for the same occasion | what was decided before — **input to refresh, never the answer**; its numbers are re-checked against today's facts | "is this the same campaign?" — say what you found and that you'll refresh it |

When sources disagree (e.g. saved notes vs the live pricing page), use the live site and say which you used in one line.

Margin is looked up (the measurement contract, the last plan); if it's not there, it is a labelled assumption marked "confirm first" in the plan — ask it only when the whole plan turns on it.

Tracking is never a technical question. If a weekly read shows how buyers are attributed, use it; if not and the goal is paid conversions, ask the one plain-words question above; the paid run checks the setup itself before any money moves.

## 2. The first message

Shape: what you found (2–3 lines — the rest belongs in the plan), then at most four questions in this order, in plain sentences — never a numbered form with sub-bullets:

1. **Product and offer.** Propose from what you found and ask to confirm; for a one-product brand, state the product and ask only about the offer.
2. **Who it's for: new people, or people who already know you** (past customers, free or lapsed users, the list), and roughly how many could be reached. This decides whether the plan is a cold-acquisition plan or a win-back plan, so it comes before the money.
3. **Goal, dates, budget** — one sentence, skip what they've said.
4. **What the plan covers** — say it, don't ask a question: every channel that could work (ads, email, organic, creators, founder outreach), kept only if it fits; they can say "ads only".
5. **At most one more gap that changes the plan** (e.g. whether a discount is possible), with why it matters and what you'll assume. When the goal is sales or paid accounts and nothing on file shows how buyers are attributed, the plain-words question is: "When someone upgrades, can you see what brought them?"

Example — a fictional brand, Fernly (invoicing app for freelancers), after the look-first pass. Never copy a real brand's facts from an example; use only what the look-first pass found.

> I had a look first: Fernly is one product, on Free, Solo ($12) and Studio ($29) monthly plans, with no offer on the site right now. Your ad account isn't connected here, and the only past campaign was a spring awareness push with no results on file.
>
> Is this for the paid plans, at full price, or is there an offer? And who is it for — new freelancers, or people who already signed up and drifted off? Roughly how many of those could you email?
>
> What's the goal (say, new paying accounts), the dates and the budget? I'll look at every channel that could work — ads, email, posts, a personal note from you to past users — and keep only the ones that fit. Say if you only want ads.
>
> Last one: when someone upgrades, can you see what brought them? If not, I'll plan around sources we can count, like tagged links and codes.

What the example does **not** ask: funnel rates, churn, margin, who makes the ads, pixels or link tags, platforms — those come from the look-first pass, or become labelled assumptions ("I've used a typical 3–5% of free users becoming paid; replace it when you know yours").

## 3. After the answer

- Only product, audience, goal, budget and date block planning. Everything else unanswered becomes a labelled assumption in "What we couldn't check".
- A follow-up question is allowed only when the answer changes the plan's shape (e.g. "1,000+ past users" turns a cold-ads plan into a win-back plan) — ask it in one line, with the plan you'll make either way.
- If the marketer pushes back on a question ("I don't know", "what kind of question is this?"), drop it, use a labelled default, and keep going.
