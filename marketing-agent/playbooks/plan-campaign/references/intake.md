# Intake — look first, then ask like a person

Load in Before you start. The first hand test failed here: the agent asked a seven-part form (funnel rates, churn, margin, tracking, hours…) before checking what GooseWorks already knew, never asked which product or offer, and asked a technical question the founder couldn't answer ("Does the signup event fire, and are UTMs in place?"). A good marketer does the opposite: reads everything they can, then asks three or four plain questions.

## 1. Look first (free, before the first message)

| Look at | It answers | Don't ask about it if found |
|---|---|---|
| `brand_read` products (with prices for the hero products) | what's for sale, plans and prices | which product exists, what it costs |
| `brand_read` onboarding | who makes the ads, who runs them, platforms used, spend band, stated goals | "who makes the ads?", "which platforms?" |
| `brand_read` learnings (full) + `knowledge_search` for the task | past results, offers that ran, rules | "what worked before?" |
| `meta_read` pages + performance | is Meta connected; recent spend and results on ads GooseWorks made | "do you run Meta ads?" |
| `social_account_list` | which organic accounts exist | "do you post on Instagram/TikTok?" |
| `campaign_read` + `ads_creative_read` (approvals view) | campaigns already planned, offers and audiences used, creative waiting | duplicates; "is this the Halloween one?" — say what you see instead |
| `lifecycle/program.md`, `lifecycle/calendar/<YYYY-MM>.md` | email/SMS list size, flows, sends already planned, standing offer | "do you have an email list?" |
| `reports/performance/definitions.md` + newest weekly read | margin, the real number (store / CRM), tracking state, recent results | margin, "is tracking set up?" |
| the last approved plan's money and status sections | AOV, margin, list size, founder hours given last time | anything already stated |
| research files (market read, personas, customer words, demand; competitor report, angle bank) | who buys, why, what rivals run | "who's your customer?" |
| the brand's public site (host fetch / browser): product and pricing pages | live offer, prices, positioning | "is there a sale on?" — confirm instead of asking |

When sources disagree (e.g. saved notes vs the live pricing page), use the live site and say which you used in one line.

Margin is looked up (the measurement contract, the last plan); if it's not there, it is a labelled assumption marked "confirm first" in the plan — ask it only when the whole plan turns on it.

Tracking is never a question. If a weekly read shows tracking state, use it; if not, note "tracking not checked yet — the paid run checks it before any money moves".

## 2. The first message

Shape: what you found (2–3 lines — the rest belongs in the plan), then at most four questions in this order, in plain sentences — never a numbered form with sub-bullets:

1. **Product and offer.** Propose from what you found and ask to confirm.
2. **Goal, dates, budget** — one sentence, skip what they've said.
3. **What the plan covers** — say it, don't ask a question: every channel that could work (ads, email, organic, creators), kept only if it fits; they can say "ads only".
4. **One or two gaps only the marketer knows that change the plan** (e.g. how many free or past users they could email; whether a discount is possible). Each says why it matters and what you'll assume if they don't know.

Example (GooseWorks, after the look-first pass):

> I had a look first: you sell one product, GooseWorks, on Free, $99, $299 and $599 monthly plans, with no offer on the site right now. Meta isn't connected here yet, and there are three earlier awareness campaigns with no results.
>
> Is this campaign for the paid plans — and is there an offer, or full price? What's the goal (say, a number of new paying accounts), the dates, and the budget?
>
> I'll look at every channel that could work — ads, email to people who already signed up, LinkedIn and X posts — and keep only the ones that fit. Say if you only want ads.
>
> One more thing that changes the plan a lot: roughly how many people have signed up before, and can you email them? If you don't know, I'll plan for new people only and say so.

What the example does **not** ask: funnel rates, churn, margin, who makes the ads, tracking, platforms — those come from the look-first pass, or become labelled assumptions ("I've used a typical 3–5% of free users becoming paid; replace it when you know yours").

## 3. After the answer

- Only product, goal, budget and date block planning. Everything else unanswered becomes a labelled assumption in "What we couldn't check".
- A follow-up question is allowed only when the answer changes the plan's shape (e.g. "1,000+ past users" turns a cold-ads plan into a win-back plan) — ask it in one line, with the plan you'll make either way.
- If the marketer pushes back on a question ("I don't know", "what kind of question is this?"), drop it, use a labelled default, and keep going.
