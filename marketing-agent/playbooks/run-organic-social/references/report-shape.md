# The social plan report

Written and delivered through `../../../atoms/write-report/ATOM.md`. Follow its rules: summary first, plain words, a fact always has a date and link, and no ids, paths, tool or playbook names. This file says what goes in each part for a social plan. The draft (step 2) and the final (step 6) have the same shape; the draft marks what's still pending.

**Title:** "<Brand> — social plan for <platforms>, <date>". Example: "Gooseworks — X plan, 18 Oct 2026".

## In short (3–5 bullets)
- **Do this first:** the one move, and why. Example: "Start replying to these 12 accounts every evening before you post: at zero followers, replies are where you get seen."
- The biggest thing the platform research found, with its so-what. Example: "X is pushing native video harder than text right now, so half your posts are 30-second screen recordings."
- What's working in your niche there, in one line.
- What you'll post, how often, and how long it takes you a week.

## Per platform (one section each)
1. **How <platform> works right now:** 3–5 dated, linked facts that shape the plan, each with what it means for you. Say how sure we are.
2. **What's getting watched in your space:** a short table of the patterns (pattern · one example with link · why it works · our version). Then the "everyone does this, so we won't" line.
3. **Your account today:** handle, followers, what has worked so far, bio and pinned post (with suggested rewrites), and voice in one line.
4. **The strategy:** the job of the account, topics, the format mix, hooks, cadence and posting times (in the audience's time zone and yours).
5. **How you break in:** the named accounts to reply to (linked), the daily routine with minutes, the first posts, and one borrowed-audience move.
6. **How we'll know it's working:** the signals for your account size, and the format tests with what each one checks and when we read it, described in words.

## The posts
- The next three days in full, in the order they go out.
- Each post opens with a line saying what it is: "Tuesday, 8:30 pm IST (9 am New York) — screen-recording video, modelled on @x's 'watch it fix this' clips ([example](link))".
- Then the post exactly as it goes out: text ready to paste, or the video script table with on-screen text and shot notes, or the slides.
- Then one line for what you need to do (record this screen, fill in this number) and any approval it needs ("needs your yes: it's in your voice").
- **All posts** (an appendix at the end of the report): every other post of the two weeks, in posting order, in the same readable shape: the what-it-is line, the post exactly as it goes out, and what you need to do. The workspace calendar keeps the machine copy; the marketer never needs to open it.

## Posting sheet
One table in posting order: date, time (your time and the audience's), platform, format, first line, what to prepare, posted ✓.

## What we couldn't check
Plain lines, one each, with why: "Couldn't read your X posts: X doesn't let us read a profile's feed without a login. Paste your last 5 posts and I'll tune the voice."

## Sources
Every link used, with its date, grouped per platform.

## Never in this report
- Slot ids, test codes, bucket names, file paths, check blocks or the internal flag syntax, including `[NAME: …]` brackets. Say "needs your yes" instead of "PERSON YES NEEDED", and "fill in: <what>" for the one allowed fill-in.
- Template posts. A post with more than one fill-in is not a post: rebuild it from found material (SKILL.md step 4).
- Raw tables of everything collected.
- Process narration ("I searched…", "step 3…").
