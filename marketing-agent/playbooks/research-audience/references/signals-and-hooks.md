# Buying signals and personalisation hooks

Load at step 5 (signals) and step 6 (hooks). A signal is evidence of *timing*; fit says who, timing says why now.

## Evidence standard (non-negotiable)

Every signal and hook in a row is: **one-line fact · URL · publish date · retrieved date · why it matters now.** No URL, no signal. Re-check that job posts are still live on the day you hand the list over.

## Signals, strongest first, with freshness windows

Approximate windows from outbound practice; older than the window = drop it.

| # | Signal | Fresh for | How to see it with what we have |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A past champion/user of the seller moved to a new in-ICP company | 0–120 days | Only if the human supplies champions (customer contacts list); check their public profile for the new employer |
| 2 | New leader in the buying role | first 30–90 days | Public profile shows the new role; announcement posts; press releases via search |
| 3 | Live job post for the role the product replaces/supports, or naming the pain/tool | open now, posted ≤45 days | Search job boards and the company careers page (e.g. queries like `"<company>" "AP specialist" site:boards.greenhouse.io OR site:jobs.lever.co`) |
| 4 | Public statement of the pain (post, talk, podcast, earnings call) | ≤60 days | Search the person's recent public posts; post search on LinkedIn/X; podcast/YouTube transcripts |
| 5 | Tech change: adopted a complementary tool, dropped a competitor | ≤90 days | Job posts naming tools, integration/partner pages, public code repos for dev tools |
| 6 | Expansion: new market, location, product line, store opening | ≤90 days | Press, the company's own news page, location pages |
| 7 | Running paid ads now (budget + active marketing) | live | Meta / Google / LinkedIn / TikTok ad libraries. Strong for selling *to marketers* (agencies, creative, martech) |
| 8 | Funding round | ≤90 days | Company page funding block (often stale — confirm with a dated press source). **Weak alone**: everyone emails newly funded companies; pair it with another signal |

**Selling to retail and wholesale buyers** (consumer brands): category review / line-review calendar for the retailer (ask the human; buyers publish deadlines), new store openings, the buyer's own posts about the category, trade-show exhibitor or attendee lists, competitor brands newly stocked, a retailer's "local makers" or new-brand programme.

**Selling to local businesses / partners**: new opening, renovation, new owner, review-volume jump, running local ads, hiring.

**Noise — never count as a signal**: "growing fast", headcount growth with no function named, awards, follower counts, generic intent-topic surges, "uses Google Analytics", funding older than six months, work anniversaries, "visited our website" (we cannot see it).

## Timing points (for the 40-point block) — judgment, with anchors

Score timing by judgment, and write the reason in the row. Anchors so runs stay comparable:
- **Strength.** The strongest signals (a past champion who moved, a new leader in the buying role, a live job post for the pain) can carry the full 40 when fresh; middle signals (a public statement of the pain, a tech change, an expansion) top out around 28; weak ones (running ads, funding) around 16. Retail: a category-review window open now is strong; a store opening, a competitor newly stocked or a new-brand programme is middle; a trade-show buyer list is weak. Local and partners: a new opening or new owner is middle.
- **Recency.** A signal early in its window counts fully; one near the end of its window counts for much less — a new leader in week 2 is a different conversation from one in week 11. Say which.
- **Two signals.** Independent signals add a little, never double; cap 40.
- Worked: a job post for the pain, 14 days old → near 40 ("live, posted 14 days ago, still open"); a new VP 11 weeks in → low teens ("late in the window — budget likely set").

When outcomes come back, re-tune these anchors from what actually replied (the outcomes ledger) and say what changed.

## Personalisation hooks — one per person

A hook is a *fact* the human's first line can build on, not copy. It must connect to the pain, not just prove research happened.

Worth it (with URL): something they published or said in the last ~60 days; their own job post; their recent move; a shared verifiable connection (former employer, same integration, mutual customer the human named); a specific company event.

Worthless: "saw you went to State U", compliments on the website, "hope you're well", invented insight, anything older than six months.

**Banned (creepy or unlawful)**: family, health, religion, politics, home location, personal (non-professional) social accounts, birthdays, inferred salary, photos. If a hook needs one of these, there is no hook — write "none found".
