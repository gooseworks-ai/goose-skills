# Audience research — the craft

Load at steps 1–4. The question a founder brings is "who are our people, and how do we reach them?" A great answer lets them write a better ad, pick the right community and send the right first message *this week*. A weak one is a persona with a stock-photo name and no evidence.

## 1. Segments that are real

- **Name each segment by situation, not demographics.** "In-house growth leads at consumer brands spending $20k+/month on Meta" beats "Marketing Mary, 34". Demographics only when they change the message (a parent buying for a teen).
- **2–5 segments.** More than five means you haven't merged; one means you haven't looked.
- **How you'd recognise one** — the job title, company type, situation or behaviour that marks a person as in the segment (so ads and lists can target it).
- **Evidence per segment**: where you saw them talk, buy, hire or complain. A segment with no evidence is labelled "a guess, because…".
- **Rank** by: how many there are (any size signal — subreddit members, job post counts, directory counts), how much pain, how well our product fits, how reachable they are. Name the **primary segment** and say why. **When the brand's own facts disagree** (the site sells one story, the saved brand text another), rank on the evidence of who actually talks about and buys this kind of product, say which story the research supports, and make the choice the first decision in the closing message — don't hedge every section.
- **B2B:** role × company type × situation, with who else is in the buying decision. **Consumer:** life situation × trigger. **Marketplace:** both sides, separately. **Local:** radius × occasion.

## 2. What they want

The outcome they are hired or living for, in their terms — "launch more ads without hiring", "clear skin without a 10-step routine". Read it from job posts (what the role is measured on), their own posts and talks, review headlines ("finally I can…"), and community threads asking for help. One or two lines per segment, with the evidence.

## 3. Their problems — and how we solve them

| Problem (their words) | How often / how bad | How we solve it | Proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| "I spend Sunday nights making 20 ad variations" | 14 threads, 3 job posts name it | Batch ad generation from a product page | A signed-off claim — or "you'd need proof of hours saved first" |

- Problems come from what they say, not what we sell. Then map honestly: solved, partly solved, not solved (say so — that's a product note, not an ad).
- Rank by frequency × intensity. A problem only rivals mention is a rival's framing, not the audience's.

## 4. Their language

- **At least five verbatim phrases per segment where the sources allow**, each linked and dated, and say who is speaking (their role if stated; "role not stated" otherwise; an employer's job post is the employer's words, not the audience's). Prefer the last 12 months; older quotes are labelled with their year. If the places this audience talks were unreadable (blocked forums), give what you have, say plainly that the quotes are thin and why, and never pad with vendor or agency copy dressed as audience voice. how they describe the problem, the outcome, the alternatives, and the words they'd never use. Pull from reviews, Reddit, community threads, LinkedIn and X posts, video comments, job posts.
- **Their words vs ours:** a short table of what we say on our site vs what they say ("AI creative engine" vs "something that makes the ads for me").
- If research-market's voice-of-customer bank exists, reuse its rows for this segment and add only what's new — never re-mine what's already banked.
- A quote is audience language, never a claim we can make.

## 5. Where they hang out

Name the actual places, with evidence of size and activity (members, posts per week, episode count, attendee numbers, last post date):

| Kind | Examples to look for |
|---|---|
| Communities | subreddits, Slack and Discord groups, Facebook and LinkedIn groups, forums, Indie Hackers, Hacker News threads |
| Channels | which social platforms they post and read on (and which they don't) |
| Publications | newsletters, trade publications, blogs they cite |
| Podcasts and YouTube | shows they mention, guests they follow |
| Events | conferences, meetups, webinars, trade shows — with dates |
| People they follow | the voices they quote and reshare (not for paying — that's the creator work) |

A place is listed only when you saw this audience in it (a post, a member count, a speaker list), with the link. "LinkedIn" alone is not an answer; "the r/PPC weekly thread (110k members, 40 posts this week)" is.

## 6. How to find and reach them

For the top one or two segments, the first three moves, each with who does it, what it needs and how we'll know it worked; for lower-ranked segments one line — "not worth effort yet, because…" is a fine answer, filler moves are not:
- **To find them:** ad targeting that matches the segment (job titles, interests, lookalike seeds, keywords), search terms they type, directories and lists that hold them.
- **To reach them:** the channel and format they respond to (a Reddit answer, not an ad; a newsletter sponsorship; a conference talk), the message to lead with (their words, our proof), and the low-friction first ask.
- **Named people** only in list mode — say "I can build a list of named people for this segment" as a decision, not a default.

## 7. You vs them

How rivals talk to this audience (their homepage words, ad hooks, the communities they're active in): where we're ahead (say it louder), behind, and where everyone sounds the same (don't lead with it).

## 8. By company type

| Type | Segment dimension | Best evidence | Where they hang out — look first |
|---|---|---|---|
| B2B SaaS | role × company type × trigger | job posts, G2/Capterra reviews of tools they use, LinkedIn posts, community threads | role communities (Slack groups, subreddits), newsletters, podcasts, conferences |
| Agencies / services | buyer role × client type | client reviews, RFPs, LinkedIn posts, industry roundups | trade groups, LinkedIn, events |
| DTC / consumer brand | life situation × trigger | product reviews, Reddit, TikTok/IG comments | subreddits, TikTok and IG niches, creators they follow, forums |
| Consumer app | situation × habit | app reviews, Reddit, TikTok | subreddits, TikTok, Discord |
| Marketplace | each side separately | reviews and Q&A on both sides | seller forums, buyer communities |
| Local business | radius × occasion | Google and Yelp reviews, local groups | local Facebook groups, Nextdoor, local events |

## 9. The report (through atoms/write-report — fictional example, never reuse its values)

Aim for a report a founder reads in ten minutes: about 1,500–2,500 words. "In short" bullets are one or two sentences each — the point and why; the evidence lives in the sections. What wasn't readable goes in "What we couldn't check", never in a methods section at the top. Long tables of every quote go in an appendix after Sources.

```

- **Do this first:** answer the weekly "creative fatigue" threads in r/PPC and r/FacebookAds for the next four weeks — in-house growth leads at consumer brands ask there every week how to make more ad variations, and nobody selling a tool answers them in their words.
- **Your best audience** is in-house growth leads at consumer brands spending on Meta: they feel the pain most (creative volume), buy without a committee, and gather in three places we can reach this month.
- **Agencies are the second audience** — bigger accounts, slower to buy, and they want white-label output, which we don't offer yet.
```
