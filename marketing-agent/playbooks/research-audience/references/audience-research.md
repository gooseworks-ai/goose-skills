# Audience research — the craft

Load at steps 1–5. The question a founder brings is "who are our buyers, and how do we reach them?" A great answer lets them write a better ad, pick the right community and send the right first message *this week* — and it rests on real buyers, not on whichever websites happened to load. A weak one is a persona with a stock-photo name and no evidence.

## Customer data — the best source, asked for once

When the brand has customers, its own data beats any public read. Ask once, up front, for the export that fits the company type (never all of these — pick the one or two that matter):

| Company type | Ask for | What it settles |
|---|---|---|
| B2B SaaS / services | a customer list or CRM export: company, role or title, plan, signup date, active or cancelled — and win/loss notes, cancel reasons | who actually pays, which roles buy, who stays, why deals are won and lost |
| DTC / consumer brand | an order or customer-segment summary (no names needed): repeat vs one-time, top products, regions — and post-purchase survey answers, return reasons, support themes | who buys again, why they buy, why they return or stop |
| Consumer app | active vs churned users by plan, cancel-survey answers, in-app feedback | who sticks, why they leave |
| Marketplace | both sides' top accounts and churn reasons | which side is short, why |
| Local business | booking or visit data by occasion, "how did you hear" answers, review export | who comes back, from where |

Reading it: cluster paying customers by the situation they're in (company type × role, or life situation × trigger) and count them; read cancel and loss reasons as their own theme list (why they leave is the most under-used finding in audience work); keep only counts and patterns — never personal data — in anything saved to the brand. Customer data outranks every public source: when it disagrees with the site, the brain or the web, it wins, and the report says so.

## 1. Buyer groups that are real

- **Name each group by situation, not demographics.** "In-house growth leads at consumer brands spending $20k+/month on Meta" beats "Marketing Mary, 34". Demographics only when they change the message (a parent buying for a teen).
- **2–5 groups.** More than five means you haven't merged; one means you haven't looked.
- **How you'd recognise one** — the job title, company type, situation or behaviour that marks a person as in the group, specific enough to use as ad targeting or list criteria.
- **Buying roles** — who uses it, who pays, who approves (B2B: champion, economic buyer, user, blocker; consumer: the buyer vs the user, the gift-giver).
- **Size in buyers, not followers.** Count people or companies: directory and store counts, company counts by size band, job-post counts for the role, industry figures, the brand's own customer mix scaled up. Each input sourced or labelled an assumption, with the arithmetic shown in words ("~4,200 US Shopify stores in skincare with 10+ staff (source) × ~1 growth lead each"). A subreddit or community's member count is **reach**, never group size — most members of a big community aren't in the group.
- **Rank** on stated criteria: size, pain, fit with what we sell today, reachability. Price fit counts — a group whose budget matches our price is a fit, not a stretch.
- **When the brand's own facts disagree** (the site sells one story, the saved brand text another): customer data decides; without it, rank on who actually talks about and buys this kind of product, say which story the research supports, and make the choice the first decision.
- **B2B:** role × company type × situation. **Consumer:** life situation × trigger. **Marketplace:** both sides, separately. **Local:** radius × occasion.

## Evidence bar — before naming a lead group

Name a lead group only when at least one holds:
- the brand's own customer data shows it (the largest or best-retained paying group), **or**
- first-hand evidence from **at least 5 distinct people in the group**, from **at least 2 kinds of source** (e.g. a review site and a community), mostly from the last 18 months — none of it vendor, agency, case-study or job-post text.

Otherwise the top-ranked group is still named as the lead, labelled **"provisional — <what's missing>"**; say what evidence would settle it (the customer export, a paid read of the forums they use, five interviews) and make that the first decision. A provisional lead never sits in "In short" as a settled finding. A lead that rests on two people and a company's own blog is a guess, and the report says so.

## 2. What they want

The outcome they are hired or living for, in their terms — "launch more ads without hiring", "clear skin without a 10-step routine". Read it from customer data and win/loss notes first, then their own posts and talks, review headlines ("finally I can…"), community threads asking for help, and job posts (what a role is measured on — the employer's view, labelled so). One or two lines per group, with the evidence.

## 3. Their problems — and how we solve them

| Problem (their words) | How often / how bad | How we solve it | Proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| "I spend Sunday nights making 20 ad variations" | 14 threads, 6 reviews | Batch ad generation from a product page — solved | A signed-off claim — or "you'd need proof of hours saved first" |

- At least three problems per top group, in their words, with a count and a link. Problems come from what they say, not what we sell; map honestly: solved, partly solved, not solved (that's a product note, not an ad).
- Rank by frequency × intensity. A problem only rivals mention is a rival's framing, not the audience's.
- **Why they leave** (cancel reasons, churn notes, "went back to X" reviews) and **why we win or lose** (win/loss notes) get their own short table when the brand has them.

## 4. Their language — the quote bank

The rules for who counts as their voice, sampling, integrity and tagging are in [quote-bank.md](quote-bank.md). In the report: at least five first-hand quotes for the lead group and three for each other top group, each linked, dated and with who's speaking — or "thin" said plainly, with why (e.g. the forums they use were unreadable). Never pad with vendor, agency, case-study or job-post text. Add a short **their words vs ours** table (what we say on our site vs what they say).

## 5. Where they gather

Name the actual places, each with **evidence of activity** (members, posts per week, episode count, attendees, last post date) **and evidence that this group is there** (a post by someone in the group, a speaker list, a sponsor page naming the audience) — at least four for the lead group:

| Kind | Examples to look for |
|---|---|
| Communities | subreddits, Slack and Discord groups, Facebook and LinkedIn groups, forums, Indie Hackers, Hacker News threads |
| Channels | which platforms they post and read on (and which they don't) |
| Publications | newsletters, trade publications, blogs they cite |
| Podcasts and YouTube | shows they mention, guests they follow |
| Events | conferences, meetups, webinars, trade shows — with dates |
| People they follow | the voices they quote and reshare (not for paying — that's the creator work) |

"LinkedIn" alone is not an answer; "the r/PPC weekly thread (110k members, 40 posts this week; three growth leads asked about creative fatigue on 12 Oct)" is. A big community where the group is a small minority is reach, not a home.

## 6. How to find and reach them

Three first moves per group (inside the group's section — the report's "What to do" list is the separate, capped list of at most six), each with the channel, the message in their words, the first ask, who does it, what it needs and how we'll know it worked. For lower-ranked groups the moves are small and cheap (a test, a listen, one post) and labelled lower priority.

- **Moves rest on what this run read.** A move on a channel the run couldn't read (a blocked forum, a login-only group) is labelled "unverified — read the community and its rules before posting", and is never the first move. "Do this first" always rests on sources read and cited in the report.
- **To find them:** ad targeting that matches "how to recognise one" (titles, interests, lookalike seeds from the customer data, keywords), search terms they type, directories that hold them.
- **To reach them:** the channel and format they respond to (an answer in a thread, not an ad; a newsletter sponsorship; a talk), the message (their words, our proof), the low-friction first ask.
- **Named people** only in list mode — offer it as a decision, not a default.

## 7. You vs them, and the sibling reports

How rivals talk to this audience (their homepage words, ad hooks, the communities they're active in): where we're ahead (say it louder), behind, and where everyone sounds the same (don't lead with it). Then read the brand's latest market and competitor reports: say in one line where this report agrees or differs — especially the lead group and the first move — and why.

## 8. By company type

| Type | Group dimension | Best evidence (after the brand's own customer data) | Where they hang out — look first |
|---|---|---|---|
| B2B SaaS | role × company type × trigger | job posts, G2/Capterra reviews of tools they use, LinkedIn posts, community threads | role communities (Slack groups, subreddits), newsletters, podcasts, conferences |
| Agencies / services | buyer role × client type | client reviews, RFPs, LinkedIn posts, industry roundups | trade groups, LinkedIn, events |
| DTC / consumer brand | life situation × trigger | product reviews, Reddit, TikTok/IG comments | subreddits, TikTok and IG niches, creators they follow, forums |
| Consumer app | situation × habit | app reviews, Reddit, TikTok | subreddits, TikTok, Discord |
| Marketplace | each side separately | reviews and Q&A on both sides | seller forums, buyer communities |
| Local business | radius × occasion | Google and Yelp reviews, local groups | local Facebook groups, Nextdoor, local events |

## 9. The report (through atoms/write-report — fictional example, never reuse its values)

Write it through write-report: "In short" at most 150 words and five bullets, body under 3,000 words, a decisions box, at most six moves in "What to do" (each group's three reach moves sit in its own section and don't count), "what we're not doing", and a source-skew line in Sources. The first bullet is "Do this first: <move> — <who does it> — because <why>". Every number in "In short" keeps its link and date. "What we couldn't check" merges every group's unread sources, each with what it would change. "In short" bullets are one or two sentences each — the point and why; the evidence lives in the sections. What wasn't readable goes in "What we couldn't check", never in a methods section at the top. Long tables of every quote go in an appendix after Sources.

