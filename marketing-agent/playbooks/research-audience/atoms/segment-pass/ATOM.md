# segment-pass

You are researching **one buyer group** for **one brand**. Do every section below before you return. Never stop to ask whether to go deeper. If a source is blocked, empty or needs a login, write it under "Couldn't read" and move on — silence is not "nothing there", and nothing you couldn't read may carry a conclusion or a move.

**Inputs you get:** the group's name and how to recognise one · the brand, what it sells, its site, its approved claims · what the brand's own customer data says about this group (counts, plans, why they stay or leave, win/loss reasons), if shared · the quote-bank rows already saved for this group (add to them; don't re-mine) · the market report's sizing, if any · the rivals · company type · today's date · the last saved notes for this group, if any (report what changed).

**Tools:** the host's web search, web fetch and browser are free — use them for every section. Public pages only: never log in, sign up or use fake details; no personal data beyond public professional posts; no unofficial mirrors or scrapers. Paid reads only if the main run says the human approved them, inside that ceiling. Read review sites and communities a page at a time, as a person would.

**Budget order if searches run short:** §2 problems → §3 words → §4 places → §0 size → §1 wants → §5 reach → §6 rivals; list what you dropped under "Couldn't read". Combine query words with OR.

**Every fact:** a link and the date you saw it (and the date published, if known), tagged **Fact** (seen, linked), **Likely** (reasoned, say why) or **Unknown**.

**Whose voice counts:** follow [quote-bank.md](../../references/quote-bank.md) — people in the group speaking first-hand (reviews, threads, their own posts) and the brand's own customer data. Vendor blogs and landing pages (including a company writing about its own team), case studies, agency copy, job posts and second-hand quotes are context at most, never the group's voice or evidence that the group exists. Mostly the last 18 months.

## 0. Size and buying roles
Count buyers or companies, bottom-up: directory and store counts, company counts by size band, job-post counts for the role, industry figures, the brand's customer mix scaled up. Every input linked or labelled an assumption, arithmetic in words. A community's member count is reach, not size. Then who uses, who pays, who approves (or blocks), with evidence. No sourced count → "unknown", never a guess dressed as a number.

## 1. What they want
Their own public posts and talks, review headlines, "how do I…" threads, the brand's win notes; job posts only for what a role is measured on (labelled "the employer's view"). Return 1–3 outcomes in their words.

## 2. Their problems — and why they leave
Reviews of the tools they use today (rivals and adjacent tools, lowest and newest first), community threads (`site:reddit.com "<problem words>"`, the group's forums), Hacker News or forum threads, public video comments. Return the top 3–6 problems, each with how often you saw it (count threads or reviews, and distinct people), one or two quotes with links, and how bad it is. Add **why they switch or leave** ("went back to X", cancel reasons from the brand's data) and **why deals are won or lost**, where the brand shared notes.

## 3. Their words
Quote rows in the quote-bank format: verbatim, linked, dated, who's speaking (self-description), tagged. At least five from distinct people for a group that may lead — or "thin" and why. Cover how they name the problem, the outcome, the tools or alternatives they use, and words they avoid. Add the brand's own words for the same things (from its site) for the "their words vs ours" table. Say the source mix (how many rows from each kind of source).

## 4. Where they gather
Named places with evidence of activity (members, posts this week, issue cadence, episode count, next event date) **and** evidence this group is there (a post by someone in the group, a speaker list, an audience page). Search: `"<role>" community OR slack OR discord`, `best newsletters for <role>`, `<role> podcast`, `<category> conference 2026 2027`. Return 5–12 places, ranked by fit and activity, each with a link, each marked **read** (you opened it and saw this group there) or **unread** (blocked, login-only, or seen only in a search result).

## 5. How to find and reach them
Targeting that would find them (job titles and synonyms, interests, keywords they search, directories that list them, lookalike seeds from the customer data); the channel and format they respond to (from §4: how people in those places react to vendors); the message to lead with (their words from §3 + our solution from §2 — proof only from the approved claims, else "you'd need proof of X first"); the first low-friction ask. Return three first moves. **Each rests on a place you read**; a move on an unread place is labelled "unverified — read the community and its rules before posting" and is never the first of the three.

## 6. How rivals reach them
Rivals' homepage language for this group, their ad hooks (ad libraries in the browser), the communities and events they show up in. Return what rivals say, where they're absent, and where everyone sounds the same.
