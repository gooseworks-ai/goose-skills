# segment-pass

You are researching **one audience segment** for **one brand**. Do every section below before you return. Never stop to ask whether to go deeper. If a source is blocked, empty or needs a login, write it under "Couldn't read" and move on — silence is not "nothing there".

**Inputs you get:** the segment's name and how to recognise one · the brand, what it sells, its site, its approved claims · research-market's voice-of-customer rows for this segment, if any (reuse; don't re-mine) · the rivals · company type · today's date · the last saved notes for this segment, if any (report what changed).

**Tools:** the host's web search, web fetch and browser are free — use them for every section. Public pages only: never log in, sign up or use fake details; no personal data beyond public professional posts; no unofficial mirrors or scrapers. Paid reads only if the main run says the human approved them, inside that ceiling. Read review sites and communities a page at a time, as a person would.

**Budget order if searches run short:** §2 problems → §4 places → §3 language → §1 wants → §5 reach → §6 rivals; list what you dropped under "Couldn't read". Combine query words with OR.

**Every fact:** a link and the date you saw it (and the date published, if known), tagged **Fact** (seen, linked), **Likely** (reasoned, say why) or **Unknown**.

## 1. What they want
Job posts for the role (what it's measured on — search `"<role>" "<company type>" job` and open 3–5 recent ones), their own public posts and talks, review headlines, "how do I…" threads. Return 1–3 outcomes in their words.

## 2. Their problems
Reviews of the tools they use today (rivals and adjacent tools, lowest and newest first), Reddit and community threads (`site:reddit.com "<problem words>"`, the segment's subreddits), Hacker News or forum threads, YouTube and TikTok comments where public. Return the top 3–6 problems, each with how often you saw it (count threads or reviews), one or two quotes with links, and how bad it is.

## 3. Their language
At least five verbatim phrases, each linked and dated, covering: how they name the problem, the outcome, the tools or alternatives they use, and words they avoid. Add the brand's own words for the same things (from its site) for the "their words vs ours" table.

## 4. Where they hang out
Find the named places with evidence of activity — subreddits (members, posts this week), Slack/Discord/Facebook/LinkedIn groups (members, how to join), newsletters (subscribers if stated, issue cadence), podcasts (episode count, recent guests), YouTube channels, conferences and meetups (next date, attendees or speakers), the people they quote and reshare. Search: `"<role>" community OR slack OR discord`, `best newsletters for <role>`, `<role> podcast`, `<category> conference 2026 2027`, `site:reddit.com/r/ <topic>`. Return 5–12 places, ranked by fit and activity, each with a link.

## 5. How to find and reach them
Targeting that would find them (job titles and synonyms, interests, keywords they search, directories that list them); the channel and format they respond to (from §4: how people in those places react to vendors); the message to lead with (their words from §3 + our solution from §2 — proof only from the approved claims, else "you'd need proof of X first"); the first low-friction ask. Return three first moves.

## 6. How rivals reach them
Rivals' homepage language for this segment, their ad hooks (ad libraries in the browser), the communities and events they show up in. Return what rivals say, where they're absent, and where everyone sounds the same.
