# A worked example of the top

A fictional brand — never reuse its names, numbers or findings, least of all as facts about GooseWorks or any real brand.

```markdown
## In short
**What we looked at:** five rival inventory apps for small restaurants, 20 Oct 2026 — ads, prices, reviews, news. Buyer quotes are mostly from one review site, which over-represents complaints.
1. **Our main pitch is now in rivals' ads.** 3 of the 5 run ads saying "count stock from your phone" (confirmed, ad libraries). It no longer sets us apart.
2. **Every rival leads with named customers; we show none.** Each homepage names 3+ restaurants with a result (confirmed); 6 reviews we read ask "who else uses this?" (likely).
3. **Their buyers are angriest about surprise fees** — 9 of 25 complaints (likely; one site). A "no surprise fees" line is worth testing.
**So, do this first (because of 1 and 2):** the founder confirms by 27 Oct which customers will let us show their name and one result. Until then, no new ads on the old pitch.
```

The evidence and its limit come first; each finding says how sure; each number counts exactly what its sentence claims ("3 of the 5", not "rivals' ads"); since named customers are not confirmed, the move asks to confirm them.
