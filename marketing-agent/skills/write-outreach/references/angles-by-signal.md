# Angles by signal — what to say, to whom, when

Load at steps 1–4. A signal tells you **why now**; the angle turns it into a first line and a reason to reply. Every opener rests on the row's dated fact (signal URL and date from research-audience's `list.csv`). No fact → no personal opener.

The test for every angle: "Because <signal>, <company> now needs <outcome>." If you can't write that sentence truthfully, use the segment's default opener instead.

## New leader in the buying role

| Weeks in role | What to say |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Too early for a pitch — at most a short welcome or a LinkedIn connection; save the sequence |
| **2–8 (best)** | "New in the seat, reviewing what the team uses" — a quick win for their first quarter |
| 8–13 | Direct about value; they're acting on their plan |
| 13+ | Weak — treat as no signal unless something else is fresh |

Use their background: **came from our customer** → "bring what worked to <new company>" (strongest; only if the marketer confirmed it); **came from a rival's customer** → they know the category, compare honestly; **same industry** → name the pains they already know; **different industry** → "what works here is different"; **internal promotion** → "now that you own the budget". An outside hire beats a promotion. Never mention the predecessor. Pitch a first-quarter win, not a six-month project. A public "starting a new role" post and their previous company may be referenced; nothing else from their profile.

## Hiring for a role we replace or support

Set up once per brand (from the fit definition): roles the product **replaces**, **augments**, roles that **buy**, **champion** and **use**.

| The post | Angle | Opener idea |
|---|---|---|
| A role we'd replace, "first hire" / "build from scratch" | Ready before the hire | "Before your new <role> starts…" — to the budget owner it can be cost against salary; to the hiring manager, never "don't hire" |
| Several posts for the role, or "scaling" | Multiply the hire | "Hiring three <roles> usually means <pain> is growing faster than the team" |
| A role we support, recent | Accelerate while you hire | "Your new <role> starts with <outcome>, not a blank page" |

Word it by the post's age: days 1–7 "before you start interviewing"; 7–14 "while you're meeting candidates"; 14+ "before you make an offer". **Who:** the hiring manager first (they own the problem; one level above the role); a separate note on cost against salary to their budget owner a few days later. Never write "you don't need to hire" — say "your new hire is effective from day one" or "complements the hire". Re-check the post is still live the day you send.

## Champion moved (a past user or buyer at a new company)

| They were | Now | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer | Buyer level | Re-sell: "same problem, new company?" |
| Buyer | Higher | Executive re-sell |
| Champion | Now owns the budget | Upgrade: "you pushed for us — now it's your call" |
| Champion | Same level | Lateral: replicate what worked |
| Power user | Any | Bottom-up: "your team will need <thing> again" |
| Lost-deal contact | Any | Fresh start: "different company, different needs" |

Warm tone, name exactly how you know each other, use what they saw at the old company (only facts the marketer confirms). Best within 30 days of the move; fine to 60. LinkedIn often suits it better than email. Never guess their new email — research-audience finds a route or the marketer has one.

## Switch intent ("alternative to X", "moving off X", "X is too expensive")

Fastest of all: within days of the post. Lead with their own words ("you mentioned <rival> gets expensive past <n> seats" — only what they wrote, linked). Honest about the difference; never bash the rival. Ask one question about what they need instead. A post in a public forum may be referenced only when it was written under their real name; never link a pseudonymous account to a person; don't quote a private group.

## Rival's public customer (case study, testimonial author)

They already buy the category. Don't pretend not to know: "I saw <company> in <rival>'s case study on <topic>". Offer a specific difference that matters to their situation, or a benchmark, not a takedown. Lower urgency than switch intent.

## Event (speaker, sponsor, exhibitor)

Before the event: "Will you be at <event>? Your talk on <topic>…" — offer to meet. After: reference the talk or topic, within a week. Sponsors and exhibitors have budget for the space. Never imply you attended if you didn't.

## News (launch, expansion, acquisition, regulation, a public post about the pain)

**Direct** (the news names our problem) → move fast; the first vendor looks informed, the fifth looks templated. **Adjacent** (the need is implied) → problem → agitate → solve. **Thematic** → awareness only; prefer another signal. Sensitive news: layoffs → empathy only, or skip; a breach, recall or lawsuit → skip unless we fix that exact thing, and never the same day; an earnings miss → never in the subject; wait 48–72 hours after any bad news. Test: would you say it to them face to face at a conference?

## Funding (only with another signal)

Talk about what the money is for (hiring, growth, new markets), **never open on the amount** or "congrats on the raise" — every vendor does. Best within 2–4 weeks of the announcement; cooling after 60 days. Early rounds fit smaller plans; late rounds enterprise.

## Running ads now (selling to marketers)

The ad library is public evidence: "<n> ads live, most started over 60 days ago" is a fact; "your ads are fatigued" is a guess — say it as a question. Link the library page. Never criticise their creative.

## Developer activity (opened issues, contributed to a rival's or a category repo)

Speak to the use case their issue or PR shows, not to their "interest". Three or more people at one company active on the same repos = the company is adopting: write to the buyer (engineering or product lead), and mention the team's activity in general terms, never by naming individual developers. Never use a commit email.

## Win-back (churned business accounts)

In range: left 3–18 months ago. Before writing, know what has shipped since, which churn reasons are fixed, and any offer.

| Why they left | Angle |
|---|---|
| Price | The new plan or price |
| Missing feature | It shipped — show it |
| Too complex | What we simplified |
| Went to a rival | What we've built since; an honest comparison |
| No results | New proof (signed off) |
| Champion left | Find and write to the new owner |
| Downsized | If they're growing again, the timing changed |

**Priority score** = change since they left (1–5) × how fixable the reason is (1–3) × account value (1, 1.5 or 2) ÷ time decay (1 at 3–6 months, 1.2 at 6–12, 1.5 at 12–18). **High ≥ 8:** personal sequence plus a founder note; **medium 4 to under 8:** personal sequence; **low under 4:** a short batch email or skip. High sequence: day 0 an update (one line acknowledging they left, no guilt, then the fix for their reason) · day 7 proof from a similar company (signed off) · day 14 an easy way back · day 28 a polite close with something genuinely useful. Consumer lapsed customers are email-lifecycle's.

## No signal

Use the segment's sharpest pain from the quote bank, said as they say it, with a question. Say "no personal hook" in the row; never fake one.
