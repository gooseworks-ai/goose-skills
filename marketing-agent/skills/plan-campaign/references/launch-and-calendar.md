# Launches, calendar and lead times

Load at step 6.

## Launch tier (set it first — it sizes everything)

| Tier | When | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **T1 — big** | New product line, new category, a hero drop the whole year leads to | Full sequence: pre-launch tease and waitlist, creators seeded, PR, list + paid + organic on the day, sustain for weeks |
| **T2 — medium** | New flavour/colourway/plan, meaningful feature | List + organic + paid on existing structure; creators if already in motion |
| **T3 — quiet** | Small improvement, restock, minor feature | Email/changelog, a post, an ad refresh; no new channels |

Tier by how much the customer's life changes, not by how hard the team worked. The tier is the marketer's call — propose it with the reason.

## Phases

1. **Pre-launch** (T-6 weeks → T-1): message written and said to a few real customers (human step); waitlist / pre-orders; creator briefs and seeding; tease content; tracking QA; the offer live on the landing page (with any code); enablement for support, sales or retailers.
2. **Launch day**: channels fire in sequence — list first (cheapest, warmest), organic, creators going live, then paid.
3. **48-hour watch**: first read on the leading indicators; fix confusion fast (page, offer, stock).
4. **Sustain**: paid scales what converted; email catches non-openers; retargeting once audiences exist.
5. **Debrief** (T+30): what moved the number, what to repeat — becomes a learnings candidate for analyze-performance.

Always-on (non-launch) campaigns skip 1–3 and run as monthly cycles of sustain + tests.

## Lead times (rules of thumb — use the makers' real turnaround if known)

| Asset / leg | Lead time |
|---|---|
| Static ads | 2–5 days (make-static-ads) |
| Video ads | a few days with the goose-video skill (priced and approved there); 5–10 days made outside GooseWorks (the marketer's team or a freelancer; the only route in local mode) |
| Product photos | a few days with the goose-product-photos skill, from real product photos; 1–2 weeks for a shoot |
| Creators | 3–6 weeks to find, vet, brief, ship product, post |
| PR / long-lead press | 4–8 weeks (gift guides: 3–5 months) |
| SEO content | 3–6 months to rank — supporting pages only for a near launch |
| Email/SMS sequence | 1–2 weeks to write, build and QA |
| Tracking QA (pixel, conversion events, UTMs, codes) | done by T-7 |
| Offer live on the landing page (price, any code, end date), checked | done by T-7; no paid launch before it |
| Platform ad review | 1–2 days; plan creative live by T-3 |
| Landing page | 1–2 weeks if new |

## Building the calendar backwards

Start at the date, place each asset's live slot, subtract its lead time to get its brief date. Any asset whose brief date is already in the past is **flagged** (and its leg re-scoped or cut) — never silently kept.

Example (T = 1 Mar):

| Date | What | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| T-35 (25 Jan) | Creator briefs out; product shipped · new product's record + photos in GooseWorks (if not in the catalog) | work-with-creators → human · human |
| T-30 | New product's claims signed off (if none approved yet), through the claims check in the first maker job | founder |
| T-28 | Static briefs to the maker; video briefs to the goose-video skill or whoever makes the video | make-static-ads · video ads |
| T-21 | Creative locked; claims checked | makers |
| T-14 | Launch email sequence built | email-lifecycle |
| T-10 | Tease email + waitlist post | email-lifecycle, run-organic-social |
| T-7 | Tracking QA: events, UTMs, codes · offer live on the landing page (with any code), checked | human / run-paid-campaigns launch check |
| T-3 | Paid creative in review | run-paid-campaigns |
| T-0 | List → organic → creators → paid | all legs |
| T+2 | 48-hour read (pacing, rejections, early CTR) | analyze-performance (pulse) / run-paid-campaigns |
| T+14 | Test T1 called | analyze-performance |
| T+30 | Debrief — the next weekly read covers it; learnings proposed there | analyze-performance |

## The buyer's calendar (not only yours)

A campaign is timed to when the **buyer** has the problem, budget and attention — which is often not the seller's date. Before fixing the window, name the buyer's busy moments in it from the personas and research (never assume them):
- A tool sold to DTC marketers: their Black Friday / Cyber Monday creative crunch runs through November, with creative locked days before — a "get your BFCM ads made" push must land before their lock, not on Thanksgiving.
- Accountants and freelancers: year-end and tax season; schools: term start; B2B buyers: budget planning in Q4, quiet weeks in late December.
- Retail buyers and consumers: the retailer's or the category's own peaks.
Write the buyer's moments as calendar rows, and say in In short when the seller's date and the buyer's moment disagree, with which one the plan follows and why.

## Collisions to check

- The brand's promo calendar: a full-price launch inside 30 days of a sitewide sale trains customers to wait.
- Seasonal CPM swings: Q4 / Black Friday CPMs can run 1.5–2× normal; plan paid tests outside peak where the date allows.
- Other campaigns live at the same time competing for the same list and audiences.
- Stock arrival dates vs launch date.
