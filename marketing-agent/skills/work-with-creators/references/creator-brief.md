# The one-page creator brief

Load at step 7. One brief per creator — a generic brief sent to everyone is the commonest failure. The brief tells the creator what must be true and what must not be said, then gets out of the way: over-scripted content dies.

## Sections (in this order, one page)

1. **Header** — creator, brand, product, campaign (if any), go-live window.
2. **Why you** — one specific line about a past post of theirs (shows the brief was written for them).
3. **Goal and audience** — one line each, in plain words.
4. **Key message** — one sentence, built only from signed-off claims or neutral facts (the kit's value props and the brain's unsigned "approved" claims are context, not claims).
5. **Product facts** — at most three, **neutral** facts from the product records: format, size, key ingredients, how to use, price (country of origin is a regulated claim, not a neutral fact). SaaS and apps: features, integrations, plan price from the product records; none on file → ask the human for 2–3. Product-page copy often carries efficacy language ("fights breakouts", "kills bacteria") — that is not a signed-off claim and does not go in a brief.
6. **Hook angles** — 2–3 *angles* written for this creator's format and style (a stitch, a routine, a "things I wish I knew"). Not scripts, not lines to read. Same claims rule as the key message.
7. **Must-says** — at most three, each a **signed-off claim** in its exact words, cleared through the `claims-gate` skill (`../../claims-gate/SKILL.md`) (it reads the sign-off; with none, it puts the few lines this brief needs to the human for a yes). Lines the human doesn't approve are cut and listed with what proof would restore them; the brief then reads "not ready until you approve: <lines>" only if its message depends on them. **Can cite (optional):** press quotes only when they are among the signed-off claims — quoted as the publication's words, never as the creator's experience. Ratings and review counts are claims: signed-off and current, or cut. Policies (shipping, returns) only from the brand's `must` facts.
8. **Never-says** — claims that are not signed off (in regulated categories the creator's **own results** are claims too — "it cleared my breakouts" is an acne drug claim: signed off or cut; acne words: clears / treats / prevents acne or breakouts), the brand's banned words, disease/medical words for regulated categories, competitor names, anything the brand's operating rules forbid.
9. **Mandatories** — disclosure (below), handles to tag, `[LINK]` / `[CODE]` placeholders the human fills when deals are agreed.
10. **Deliverables and specs** — platform, format, length, aspect ratio, count, posting window, minimum time live. **Newsletters:** placement (primary, secondary or dedicated send), word count, who writes the copy (usually the writer, from talking points), send date, tracked `[LINK]`. **Podcasts:** host-read talking points (not a script), pre- or mid-roll, length, promo URL or code placeholder.
11. **Timeline** — product arrives (if gifted), draft due, feedback within ~48h, live date. Realistic: rush costs extra.
12. **Approval** — one round, covering claims and disclosure only.
13. **Usage rights (proposed, pending contract)** — organic repost term; paid usage / whitelisting / partnership-ad access and term; exclusivity window if wanted. The human negotiates; the brief only states the ask.
14. **What not to do** — 2–4 lines (e.g. "don't read the product facts to camera", "no before/after claims").
15. **Creative freedom** — "Your voice, your edit. We only check claims and disclosure."
16. **Two examples** — (a) one of **this creator's own** posts that already fits the brief; (b) one category winner for the *format* — prefer a creator's post that does not feature a competitor's product; never a brand ad to copy.
17. **Contact** — `[placeholder — the human fills this in]`.

## Seeding variant (gift-only, no obligation)

A gifted creator owes nothing; a paid-deal brief in a $20 box reads as a demand and gets ignored. Keep it to a short note:
- **Why you** — one specific line about their content.
- **What's in the box and how to use it** — neutral facts.
- **If you post** — 1–2 angle ideas in their format; no required deliverable, no draft, no approval round, no deadline.
- **Disclosure if you post** — "#gifted" (or "thanks to <brand> for sending") in the first line and said in the video; the platform's label where it applies.
- **Tag** — the brand's handles.
- **Never-says** — the claims the creator must not make for the brand (disease words, anything not signed off).
- **Rights** — only permission to repost on the brand's own accounts with credit; paid usage is negotiated later if the post performs.

## UGC-for-ads variant (the brand owns, edits and runs the asset)

Use this shape instead of sections 6, 10, 12 and 15 when the role is UGC-for-ads (keep 11, the timeline, and add when the product or app access arrives — before filming):
- **Concepts and hooks** — 1–2 concepts, each with **2–3 hook variants as separate deliverables** (the brand tests hooks).
- **Shot list** — e.g. face-to-camera open, product or screen-recording close-ups, the moment of use, a reaction; lighting and sound basics.
- **CTA** — the ask at the end, in plain words.
- **Deliverables** — finished cuts per hook in 9:16 (and 4:5/1:1 if asked), plus **raw footage** (priced in the deal), no music baked in if the brand adds its own.
- **Revisions** — one round on the creative itself, not only claims and disclosure.
- **Freedom statement** — "Be yourself on camera; we edit from your raw footage."
Everything else (must-says, never-says, honest-use line, rights ask, timeline, examples) stays. **Hook variants and the CTA are lines said on camera in the brand's own ads** — the same claims rule applies to every one of them: signed-off claims or neutral facts only.

## Disclosure (state the requirement; the human owns legal sign-off)

**UGC-for-ads variant**: when the asset runs from the brand's own ad account, the ad is the brand's and is labelled as an ad by the platform — the creator does not add #ad to a post they never publish — but if the brand's ad presents the creator as an ordinary customer, US endorsement rules may require the paid connection to be disclosed in the ad itself: flag it for the human or legal. When it runs from the creator's handle (Spark / partnership ads, whitelisting), the platform's branded-content or partnership label applies. Either way, the creator's words must reflect honest use: no claimed usage duration ("I've used it for months") or results they have not had; for a new product or app, they use it before filming.

The platform and market rules (paid-partnership labels, where "#ad" / "#gifted" goes, spoken disclosure, LinkedIn, newsletters, podcasts, US and UK rules) come from the shared `../../ad-policy-check/disclosure.md` (in the `ad-policy-check` skill) — load it and copy the lines for this creator's platform and market into **Mandatories**. Brief-specific additions: gifted content needs disclosure too; LinkedIn rights ask = permission to run the post as a Thought Leader ad; markets the shared file doesn't cover → "check local rules", never a guess.

## Example (FICTIONAL brand and creator — the bar, not data)

> **Tailwag Joint Chews × @fictional_corgimom** (TikTok, 38k) · live Nov 14–20
> **Why you:** your "senior corgi on the stairs" video — your audience is people with ageing dogs.
> **Goal:** awareness among owners of dogs 7+. **Key message:** an easy daily chew that supports healthy joints in older dogs.
> **Facts:** chicken-flavoured soft chew · one a day by weight · 60 chews a tub.
> **Hook angles:** Biscuit's morning routine, then the chew as one beat in it · "things I wish I'd started when she turned 7" · stitch a vet-tips video with your own experience.
> **Must-say:** "supports healthy joints" · "vet-formulated" (both signed off).
> **Never-say:** "cures", "pain relief", "arthritis treatment", any rival brand.
> **Mandatories:** paid-partnership toggle + #ad in line one + said in the first 3 seconds · tag @tailwag · [LINK].
> **Deliverable:** 1 vertical video, 30–60s, live ≥30 days. **Timeline:** chews arrive Nov 3 · draft Nov 10 · our notes within 48h · live Nov 14–20.
> **Approval:** one round, claims and disclosure only. **Usage (proposed, pending contract):** 30-day organic repost; 30-day Spark Ads access.
> **Don't:** read the facts to camera; show a before/after mobility claim.
> **Freedom:** your voice, your edit. **Examples:** your "stairs" video [url]; a top category post for format [url].
> **Contact:** [placeholder]

## Why briefs fail

Scripts; more than three must-says; creators presenting long use or results they haven't had; feature dumps; one brief for everyone; claims that are not signed off; asking for the brand's voice instead of the creator's; vague deliverables or no posting window; no disclosure line; unrealistic turnaround; several feedback rounds; no rights ask (then the content can't be reused in ads).
