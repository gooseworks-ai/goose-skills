# The playbook map

Load at step 4. One entry per playbook (the heading is its internal name; the founder only ever hears the "Say it as" line): what the marketer says, the situation it fits, what it produces, what the founder must bring, what it reads from other playbooks, and what it does not do. Recommend only from this list. When a need has no playbook, say so plainly (see "Gaps" at the end).

How to use the "must bring" line: if the founder cannot bring it today, that playbook is not a first move. Recommend whatever unblocks it, or a different playbook. The "Costs" line is checked against what the founder said they can spend: a founder with $0 can still run the free playbooks, and a paid step always waits for a yes: to the price GooseWorks quotes for made work, or to a credit ceiling for public-data reads.

## Claims before copy (not a playbook)
- Whenever a recommended playbook writes copy that states benefits, results, health, ingredient or allergen facts and the brain has no "Claims sign-off" rule (common in regulated categories), name it under "Bring": "about 5 minutes to OK the few claim lines the first job needs — I'll ask inside that job." The maker's claims check puts those 3–5 lines to the founder; when the Brand Brain is on, they confirm them in the GooseWorks app. Picks that work from plain facts (listing and profile fixes, research) don't need it.
- Learning the whole brand in one pass (every claim audited, the voice signed off, a brand book read) is not covered yet. When brand facts themselves are the constraint (the founder can't say what they sell, to whom, or what they may claim), the first move is a human step: write down those three things. research-audience helps alongside.

## research-market — "Which market are we in, how big is it, and what's changing?"
- **Say it as:** "researching your market: which category you compete in, how big it is, what's changing, how people buy and what they pay — and what people are saying about you" (never the name above, to the founder).
- **Fits:** before positioning, a plan or a raise; when the category is moving (platform changes, new entrants, price shifts); "what are people saying about us?" (a narrow ask covers just that); "watch my brand" (alerts only, weekly or daily during a launch or crisis).
- **Produces:** a market report — which market to compete in, its size top-down and bottom-up (buyers × price, from research-audience's buyer groups), what's changing, how and where buyers find and buy, pricing norms, where attention is, demand and seasonality, what people are saying (mentions, sentiment, trends with post-by dates, risks), the competition in brief, what it means for positioning; a demand read; or brand-watch alerts. A serious risk is told in chat at once.
- **Must bring:** nothing — it researches first.
- **Reads:** the brand brain; research-audience's buyer groups; the latest competitor report.
- **Not:** who the buyers are and their words (research-audience), rival teardowns, positioning, posting or replying (reply drafts are not covered yet).
- **Costs:** Free first; paid reads (full social counts, platforms behind logins) only within one credit ceiling the founder approves after the report.

## research-competitors — "What are my competitors running right now, and what works for them?"
- **Say it as:** "looking at what your competitors are running and what works for them" (never the name above, to the founder).
- **Fits:** before briefing ads, before a planning cycle, when a competitor visibly moves; categories with active advertisers (DTC, apps).
- **Produces:** a competitor report (the frame we compete in, timely openings, rivals one by one, pricing and what a typical buyer actually pays, how they compare, formats and angles, what to do), rival records with snapshots, an angle bank, and an optional weekly what-changed report.
- **Must bring:** nothing — rivals are chosen from category evidence; the founder can add or drop them afterwards.
- **Reads:** the brand brain and its tracked rivals, the latest market report, research-audience's buyer groups.
- **Not:** making ads, budget calls, mining customer reviews, brand listening.
- **Costs:** Free first; paid reads (other ad libraries, social mentions) only within one credit ceiling the founder approves after the report.

## research-audience — "Who are our buyers, in their own words, and how do we reach them?"
- **Say it as:** "finding out who your buyers are, what they want and struggle with, the words they use and where to reach them" (never the name above, to the founder).
- **Fits:** the founder doesn't know who buys and why; messaging feels generic; before positioning, campaigns or ads; a new segment appears. A named list of businesses or people to contact only when asked.
- **Produces:** buyer groups (how to recognise each, size, who uses, pays and approves), the voice-of-customer bank (sourced quotes), an audience report with where each group gathers and how to reach it; on request, a fit-scored list with sourced contact routes.
- **Must bring:** one thing, if the brand has customers: its own customer data (a list or export, win/loss or churn notes) — the one question allowed up front.
- **Reads:** the brand brain; the latest market and competitor reports.
- **Not:** market size and trends (research-market), writing or sending outreach, creator discovery.
- **Costs:** Free first; people and email lookups in list mode spend credits within a ceiling the founder approves.

## plan-campaign — "I have this budget and a launch date — what do I run, where?"
- **Say it as:** "planning the campaign: where to run, what to spend, on which dates" (never the name above, to the founder).
- **Fits:** a real budget and a real date (launch, drop, season); monthly replans once things are live.
- **Produces:** a campaign brief (goal chain, channel decisions, budget split, calendar, test plan, angle per channel) and an asset list, recorded as a campaign.
- **Must bring:** the budget, the date, a goal (it offers options if there is no number), channels already committed, anything off-limits. A pre-launch brand with a real launch date is its launch mode: earned attention first, no paid before launch, paid only after a first read, a calendar working back from about six weeks out. (where-to-start's own view: a launched company still searching for fit, with no channel shown to work, usually isn't ready for a plan yet.)
- **Reads:** the brand brain; research-market and research-competitors outputs (it recommends them if empty); the weekly performance read when replanning.
- **Not:** research, making assets, moving money, negotiating the goal.
- **Costs:** Free.

## make-static-ads — "I need ten fresh ads this week and I have no designer."
- **Say it as:** "making a set of image ads" (never the name above, to the founder).
- **Fits:** paid social is live or about to be, and creative is the bottleneck; DTC especially.
- **Produces:** a set of ready-to-run static ads, each with its angle, claims check and policy check. Costs credits (the price is shown first).
- **Must bring:** which product, how many, where they will run, any offer; signed-off claims (or 3–5 claim lines confirmed in the chat); the brand's research complete and product images in the brain.
- **Reads:** the brand brain; angle bank, voice-of-customer bank, campaign brief and performance read when they exist.
- **Not:** launching, budgets, video, landing pages.
- **Costs:** Spends credits per ad; the price is shown first.

## run-paid-campaigns — "Take the winner, scale it, kill the loser."
- **Say it as:** "running your live ads: what to scale, pause or swap each week" (never the name above, to the founder).
- **Fits:** ads are live and spending daily; the founder makes the same judgment calls by hand every morning.
- **Produces:** a weekly moves proposal (kill, scale, pause, rotate) staged for the founder's yes, and a change log. Every money move is approved by the human.
- **Must bring:** a live ad account (Meta has real levers; other platforms get written instructions), an approved plan-campaign brief, the allowable cost per customer or lead, a budget ceiling.
- **Reads:** campaign brief, approved creative, weekly performance read.
- **Not:** making ads, choosing channels for a new campaign, explaining performance.
- **Costs:** Free to propose (scheduled weekly runs use some credits); ad spend itself is the founder's own money, every move approved.

## analyze-performance — "What worked this week, on every channel, and what do I make next?"
- **Say it as:** "reading what's working across your channels each week" (never the name above, to the founder).
- **Fits:** anything is live and producing numbers: ads, email, organic, creators. Also the fix when numbers disagree or tracking may be broken.
- **Produces:** a weekly performance read (winners, why, what to make next, real revenue beside platform claims, tracking breaks with a fix) and a daily pulse.
- **Must bring:** which revenue number is the truth (store or CRM), metric definitions (agreed once), exports from tools GooseWorks cannot read.
- **Reads:** campaign brief, change log, ad angle notes, competitor digest, research-market's "what people are saying" section.
- **Not:** applying changes, making creative, the replan itself.
- **Costs:** Free (a scheduled weekly report uses some credits).

## email-lifecycle — "Cart and welcome emails make money while I sleep — mine do not exist."
- **Say it as:** "setting up your automatic emails (welcome, cart, post-purchase) and this month's sends" (never the name above, to the founder).
- **Fits:** a store or app with a list but no welcome, cart, post-purchase or win-back flows; a pre-launch waitlist that needs a launch sequence; marketplaces (flows for both sides); local businesses (booking reminders, review requests, recall); B2B trial or demo nurture.
- **Produces:** a flow pack (triggers, timing, finished messages), a segment plan and promo calendar with this week's broadcast drafts, a deliverability report. The human loads and sends.
- **Must bring:** an email tool, how the list was collected (consent), the standing first-order offer, which flows already run.
- **Reads:** the brand brain; performance read; campaign brief.
- **Not:** sending, designing offers from scratch, list-growth popups, cold outbound.
- **Costs:** Free (writing only; the founder's email tool sends).

## improve-search-visibility — "When people ask ChatGPT or Google for products like mine, am I in the answer?"
- **Say it as:** "getting found when people ask Google or AI assistants" (never the name above, to the founder).
- **Fits:** buyers research before buying (B2B SaaS, considered purchases, services, local businesses, apps with store search); organic traffic has flattened; a competitor keeps getting named.
- **Produces:** a visibility report question by question and a this-week list of five fixes finished enough to apply (page rewrites, profile corrections, listing changes, app-store listing).
- **Must bring:** approval of the buyer questions it drafts (and the 3–5 that matter most), the list of locations for a local business, who can edit the site and profiles this week, and pasted AI answers if the host has no browser.
- **Reads:** the brand brain.
- **Not:** full articles (make-content), marketplace listings, link building, paid search.
- **Costs:** Google organic lookups are paid, within a credit ceiling the founder approves first; AI-answer checks run in the host's browser or from answers the founder pastes.

## work-with-creators — "Which creators are worth paying, at what price — and what should they make?"
- **Say it as:** "finding creators worth paying and briefing them" (never the name above, to the founder).
- **Fits:** categories creators move (beauty, fashion, food, fitness, consumer apps, games); a budget for gifting or paid posts.
- **Produces:** a vetted creator shortlist with benchmarked prices and gift-vs-paid calls, and one-page briefs. The human contacts and closes every deal.
- **Must bring:** the goal (awareness, content, tracked sales), budget, platforms, creators already in talks.
- **Reads:** research-market's saved outliers, research-audience's buyer groups, campaign brief.
- **Not:** outreach, gifting, codes, contracts, affiliates.
- **Costs:** Spends credits on creator discovery and vetting lookups, within a ceiling the founder sets.

## make-content — "Write the first pass of everything."
- **Say it as:** "writing first drafts of your content" (never the name above, to the founder).
- **Fits:** a blank page or one good asset that should be ten; B2B content and founder-led thought leadership; services firms.
- **Produces:** content briefs, first drafts to edit (not rewrite), and repurposed per-platform versions. The human publishes.
- **Must bring:** the source asset to repurpose, or the topic, audience, format and where it will publish; a real opinion or story if there is one.
- **Reads:** the brand brain; voice-of-customer bank; page briefs; campaign brief.
- **Not:** publishing, ad creative, setting content strategy.
- **Costs:** Free.

## run-organic-social — "Keep my accounts alive: calendar, posts, replies."
- **Say it as:** "keeping your social accounts alive with a two-week calendar and posts" (never the name above, to the founder).
- **Fits:** accounts that go quiet when the week gets busy; founder-led brands where the founder's own account is the channel; a new account (handle, bio, pinned post, first nine posts); a launch run-up.
- **Produces:** a two-week calendar and ready-to-paste post drafts. The human posts by hand.
- **Must bring:** which accounts (brand vs founder) and each one's job, the cadence they can really keep, anything off-limits.
- **Reads:** make-content's drafts, research-market's report (trends with post-by dates), the voice.
- **Not:** posting, scheduling, replies, listening, making media.
- **Costs:** Free by default.

## Gaps: needs with no playbook yet

Name these out loud using the "What to say" line only; backlog card names are internal and never said to the founder. Never route them to the nearest playbook as if it covered them. When the binding constraint sits here, the first move is a human step (the founder, a developer, a designer), and at most one playbook helps alongside.

| The need | Status | What to say | Helps alongside |
|---|---|---|---|
| Positioning from scratch (no clear "who it's for and why us") | write-my-positioning (backlog) | "Writing positioning isn't covered yet. You decide it; finding out who buys and their words gives you what it's built from." | research-market |
| Offers, pricing, discount design | plan-offers (backlog) | "Offer design isn't covered yet. Decide the offer; everything I make for you will use it." | email-lifecycle slots a standing offer |
| Landing page, store, checkout or onboarding fixes (CRO) | audit-my-pages (backlog) | "Page fixes aren't covered yet; you or your developer make them." | analyze-performance (a light check that top ads match their landing pages) |
| Marketplace listings (Amazon, Etsy, Walmart…) | optimize-listings (backlog) | "Listing optimization isn't covered yet." | none |
| Sending outreach, sequences, follow-ups | do-outreach (backlog) | "We build the list; you send from your own tool." | research-audience |
| Replying to comments and DMs, comment moderation | Reply drafts (backlog) | "Drafting replies isn't covered yet; you reply yourself. I can show you what people are saying." | research-market |
| Always-on alerts (spikes, disapprovals, stock-outs) | watch-and-alert (backlog) | "Standing alerts aren't built; the weekly read and daily pulse cover it for now." | analyze-performance |
| Setting up tracking, analytics, the pixel, the ad account | human or developer | "Setting up tracking is yours (or your developer's); once it exists, the weekly read checks it." | analyze-performance (tracking-health check) |
| Recruiting individual supply or freelancers (gig workers, walkers, tutors without a business presence): job boards, referral rewards from current supply | human | "Recruiting individuals isn't covered; here is the first step you take." | none (research-audience only for supply that is a business) |
| Video ads (Reels, TikTok and Shorts ads, AI UGC-style video, product demos) | the goose-video skill (GooseWorks, not a playbook) | "Video ads are made in GooseWorks' video flow; I can start it as the next job." Without GooseWorks: "I can plan what to make and write the brief for whoever makes it." | plan-campaign (the asset list), work-with-creators (real creators filming) |
| Product photos without a shoot | the goose-product-photos skill (GooseWorks, not a playbook) | "New product photos are made in GooseWorks' photo flow from your real product photos; I can start it as the next job." Without GooseWorks: "Your image ads use the photos you already have." | make-static-ads (ads from existing photos) |
| Customer interviews and surveys | human | "Talk to five customers yourself; I can tell you who and what to ask." | research-audience |
| PR and press, referrals and loyalty, community, events and trade shows, retail and trade, WhatsApp and chat, offline and local field marketing, product-led growth and onboarding loops | no playbook | "This is real work I can't do for you yet; here is the first step you take yourself." | make-content for community posts or a founder article, where it fits (it does not write press releases or pitches) |
| Product-market fit, the product itself, pricing, hiring a salesperson, raising money | not marketing | "This is not a marketing problem yet; here is why." | none. Never give investment or fundraising advice. |

## Where each company type's work sits on the map

A lens, not a route: it shows which playbooks touch each type's heaviest work (page 00 Part 4) and which of that work is off the map. The constraint still decides the pick.

| Company type | Heaviest work on the map | Heaviest work off the map |
|---|---|---|
| DTC e-commerce | make-static-ads, run-paid-campaigns, email-lifecycle, work-with-creators, analyze-performance | offers and promos, store and product-page conversion, WhatsApp; video ads and product photos go to the goose-video and goose-product-photos skills |
| B2B SaaS / sales-led | research-market, research-audience, make-content, improve-search-visibility, run-organic-social (founder account) | positioning from scratch, sending outreach, product-led onboarding |
| B2B non-SaaS, CPG in retail | research-audience, make-content | trade and distributors, trade shows, retail, sampling |
| Consumer app | improve-search-visibility (app-store listing), email-lifecycle (push and in-app), run-organic-social, analyze-performance (cohorts) | onboarding and growth loops (product work); video ads go to the goose-video skill |
| Marketplace seller (Amazon, Etsy, Walmart) | improve-search-visibility (search and AI answers off the marketplace), analyze-performance (from exports the seller pastes) | listing copy and images, marketplace ads (Sponsored Products), reviews and deals; the marketplace's own rules on claims |
| Marketplace (two-sided) | research-audience (supply that is a business), improve-search-visibility (demand side), plan-campaign | recruiting individual supply (gig workers), liquidity incentives, community |
| Local / multi-location | improve-search-visibility (local search, listings, review-request brief), research-market (what locals say on Instagram and local forums), make-content | offline and field, referrals, WhatsApp, review replies |
| Services, agency, consultant | make-content (thought leadership), research-audience, run-organic-social (founder account), improve-search-visibility | positioning, referrals, speaking, PR |
| Creator / media | run-organic-social, make-content, email-lifecycle (newsletter welcome) | sponsorship sales, memberships |
| Non-profit | email-lifecycle (donors), make-content | grants, events, PR |
| Regulated categories (health, beauty, supplements, finance, food, kids) | the claims check's sign-off of the lines each writing or making job needs, before anything states a fact | a full claims audit, legal review |
