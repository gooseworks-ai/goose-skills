# The five situation questions

Load at steps 1 and 2. Five questions cover what a senior advisor needs before naming a first move. Read the brain first; ask only what it cannot answer. Each question exists because its answer can change the recommendation.

| # | Question (plain words) | Why it changes the answer | Often already in the brain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **What do you sell, to whom, and where do people buy it?** (a store / an app / software for businesses / a local business / a service / something else, plus a link) | Company type, sales motion and where it is sold decide which playbooks are even relevant. | `brand_read` summary (description, `brandType`), products, kit `audience`; the short look at the homepage and store page |
| 2 | **Where are you right now?** (a) not launched yet; (b) launched, few or no paying customers; (c) steady sales, want more; (d) it was growing, now it's flat or falling. **And who are your customers today, and how did they find you?** (The proxy for fit — (b) are first users active every week, and would they pay? (c) what share buy again? (d) what changed when it flattened? — goes in the follow-up round, unless an ask is spare.) | Stage, the channel that already works, and the fact that separates a fit problem from a demand or conversion problem. | `ad_library_ad_count` in the brand list (own ads live), the rating line among research claims, organic posts, campaigns (`campaign_read`) |
| 3 | **What have you tried, how did it go, and what were you thinking of trying next?** Plus anything you already have: an email list of about how many, a following, a network, press, partners — **and notes from user or cancel calls: paste them.** (Ask this before ever recommending "talk to N users": the notes may already answer it.) | Avoids repeating failures; surfaces unfair advantages that beat any cold channel; the idea they bring ("maybe run some ads?") must be answered, often under "not yet". | the one `knowledge_search` across evidence, learnings, documents and creatives (own ads, offers, results), accounts, learnings |
| 4 | **What can you put in each month?** Money: $0 / under $1k / $1–5k / $5k+. Your time: about 2 / 5 / 10+ hours a week. Who else does the work, if anyone. | Feasibility. Money without time points one way, time without money another. | onboarding `ad_spend` band (a band, not a number) |
| 5 | **What would make the next 90 days a win, and is a big date coming** (a launch, a season, a fundraise, an event)? **Where do you think it's stuck?** | The goal and deadline set the urgency and the order; the founder's own guess surfaces the constraint (and is often a symptom). | `brand/situation.md` from an earlier visit, onboarding `goals` |

## How to ask

- **Open with what you know.** "Here's what I already know about you: … Correct anything." Then ask only the gaps.
- **One message, numbered, with options** plus "or say it your way". **At most five asks in total** — count every separate thing you ask, not the numbered lines: five numbered questions holding fourteen asks is fourteen. Each numbered question is one ask the founder can answer with one pick or one line; anything more waits for the follow-up round. Pick the five whose answers could change the recommendation; drop the rest. Questions 2, 4 and 5 belong to the founder and are always asked on a first visit, however much the brain holds; a rich brain only lets you skip question 1.
- **State, don't ask, what you can work out:** the season and the category's big moments from today's date (e.g. a DTC brand in October: "Black Friday is about six weeks out"), the stage when the brain already shows it.
- **Plain words.** No CAC, ICP, ROAS, MER, PMF in the questions.
- **"I don't know" is an answer.** Offer a proxy ("roughly how many orders last week?", "who were your last three customers?"); accept ranges. If the unknown decides the recommendation, make the first move the one that answers it, or give a conditional answer ("if most of your customers came back for a second order, start here; if not, start there"). State any assumption in one line.
- **At most one follow-up round,** and only for a fact that would flip the recommendation. One to ask when it does: the founder calls a group "not our audience" (e.g. people who left over price) while the best customer they describe is the most price-sensitive (solo founders) — ask who the churners actually were before anything is saved.
- **Echo the situation back** in one line before recommending, so a misread is caught before the advice.

## Never
- Ask what the founder's own message already answered, or question 1 when the brain and site answer it.
- Treat a plan, brief or research saved on the brand as the founder's answers. It came from another session; name it, with its date, and ask whether it still stands.
- Turn it into a form or more than five questions.
- Ask for passwords, account access or documents. The recommended playbook asks for what it needs.
- Research beyond the short look (at most three of their own public pages): no competitors, ad libraries or paid data. The founder's words, the brain and those pages are the only inputs.
