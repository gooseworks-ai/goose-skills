# Angles and hooks — how a senior creative strategist builds a set that actually tests something

The on-image words are the composer's (`composer.md`); the hooks here steer it through each ad's Variant brief.

## 1. Angle ≠ execution

An **angle** is a reason to buy, for a specific person, at a specific moment of awareness. An **execution** is how the ad looks (format, template, colours, headline wording). Ten ads with one angle in ten templates test nothing — the account learns one thing ten times. The set must answer: *which reason to buy moves this audience?*

Two angles are different only if **at least two** of these differ:

| Dimension | Examples |
|---|---|
| The job / desire | look put-together without effort · stop wasting money on products that break you out · sleep through the night · close the books faster |
| The enemy / obstacle | hidden pore-cloggers in "clean" products · the 3pm crash · spreadsheets · the gym you never go to |
| The person / moment | first breakout after a new product · new parent at 3am · founder at month-end close · gift-giver in December |
| The proof type | press quote · review · ingredient mechanism · founder story · number · demo |
| Awareness stage | unaware · problem-aware · solution-aware · product-aware · most-aware (see §2) (counts only when the brief covers both cold and warm audiences) |

The diversity test before writing anything: say each angle in one sentence starting "They buy because…". If two sentences say the same thing with different words, merge them. Then each pair must also differ in persona, proof or situation. No angle carries more than ~40% of the ads unless the brand has fewer reasons than that allows (say so). Awareness follows the brief rather than being spread: cold sets sit in unaware → solution-aware, retargeting sets in product- and most-aware (`creative-taxonomy.md` §3).

**Market sophistication.** In a saturated category (moisturiser, protein powder, project-management software) the plain benefit ("hydrating", "saves time") is worn out. Lead with the mechanism, a specific confirmed number, or identity. Juniors lead with the worn-out claim.

**One taxonomy for statics and video.** Tag each ad's family with the video engine's angle-type names (`type:` tag, mapping in §4) and its specific angle with the bank id, lower-case (`angle:a-07`), so analyze-performance compares angles across formats.

## 2. Awareness stages (Schwartz) — what the first line must do

| Stage | What they know | What the hook must do | Typical CTA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Not that they have the problem | Name a symptom or moment they recognise; curiosity | Learn more |
| Problem-aware | The problem, not the solutions | Agitate the problem in their words, hint at a new way | Learn more / Shop now |
| Solution-aware | Solutions exist, not yours | Show why this mechanism/approach is different | Shop now |
| Product-aware | Your product, not convinced | Proof, objection-handling, comparison | Shop now |
| Most-aware | Wants it, needs a reason now | Offer, scarcity that is real, bundle | Get offer / Shop now |

Prospecting (cold) sets lean on problem-aware and solution-aware. Retargeting sets lean on product-aware and most-aware: reviews, objections, offers. Ask which the ads are for if the brief does not say; default to prospecting.

## 3. The angle families (pick 3–5 that differ)

1. **Problem → solution.** Name the pain in the buyer's words, show the product as the fix.
2. **Mechanism / why it works.** The ingredient, feature or method that makes it different (needs a confirmed or plain product-fact basis).
3. **Social proof.** Reviews, ratings, press quotes, "as seen in" — only real, attributed, current.
4. **Us vs them / old way vs new way.** Contrast with the old way (a habit, a routine, a workaround), not with other products — "most X are greasy" or a named competitor needs a comparative claim the brain marks `confirmed`.
5. **Identity / belonging.** "For people who…" — the tribe the buyer wants to be in. Careful with personal-attribute policy (see the `ad-policy-check` skill (`../../ad-policy-check/SKILL.md`) §2).
6. **Objection handling.** Price, "will it break me out", "is it hard to set up", "does it work for my skin type" — answered with proof.
7. **Founder / origin story.** Why it exists, in the founder's words (only if the brain holds the story).
8. **Demo / feature-benefit.** Show it working; screenshots for software, texture/in-hand for physical.
9. **Offer / urgency.** Only with a real, live offer the brain or the human gives. Never invented.
10. **Curiosity / news.** New ingredient, new release, a surprising fact — must be true.
11. **Gift / occasion.** Seasonal moments, gifting, events.
12. **Listicle / reasons-why.** "3 things in every jar" — a stack of benefits, each traceable.

For a ten-ad set: 3–5 concepts × 2–3 looks each (`creative-taxonomy.md` §3) — looks differ in framework, style or composition, never only in words; an offer concept only if an offer is live.

## 5. Hooks in the customer's words

The hook is the first thing read: the headline on the image, or the first line of primary text. It decides most of an ad's fate, so write many hooks and fewer bodies.

**Mining voice-of-customer for hooks:**
- Pull exact phrases from the voice-of-customer bank (`reading-the-brain.md` §2). Look for: the moment of pain, the worry before buying, the comparison with what they used before, the switching story. Outcome phrases ("my skin calmed down") stay as pain context or need a confirmed claim — a customer quote never carries the benefit.
- Keep their syntax: "I forgot I was wearing it" beats "Our lightweight formula".
- A quote used as a testimonial must be real, verbatim, attributed and permitted; a phrase borrowed as a hook must not pose as a quote.

**Hook formulas** — every example is traced to the worked example's hypothetical (`handoff-format.md` §5: claims C1–C5, customer words V1–V5, label directions, founder story F1); in a real run each line must trace the same way to what the brand has on file:
- Situation call-out: "11am, and skin already feels tight." (V1, as pain in brand voice) — a moment stated, never a question about the viewer's trait ("Your acne…?" breaks Meta's personal-attribute rule).
- Contrarian opinion + confirmed proof: "A face cream doesn't need a scent. Fragrance-free." (opinion = puffery; proof = C2 verbatim) — never imply rivals mislabel without a confirmed comparative claim.
- Specific number: "Made with 5 ceramides." (C4 verbatim)
- Question the buyer asks: "Will it feel too heavy?" (V5's worry) — answered only with a usable fact, as recorded: "Non-greasy finish." (C3)
- Customer words first: "I forgot I was wearing it." (V2, attributed on a quote card)
- Social proof first: "Rated 4.8/5 from 12,400+ reviews." (C5 verbatim, dated)
- Old worry vs now: "Old worry: too heavy. Now: Non-greasy finish." (V5's worry, C3 verbatim) — contrast the buyer's own old habit or worry, never other products: "most X…" or a named rival needs a confirmed comparative claim, and "no more reapplying" implies a result unless a confirmed claim states it.
- Founder confession: "I wanted one cream I didn't have to think about." (F1 verbatim)
- Instruction: "Apply morning and night." (label directions, exactly) — never imply it replaces other products unless a confirmed claim says so.

**Hook rules:**
- One message. If the hook needs "and", it is two ads.
- Six to eight words on the image; more goes in the primary text (the composer also mirrors the template's text density).
- Concrete beats clever. Nouns and numbers beat adjectives.
- No claim the claims check would cut — write within the usable brain facts from the start.

## 6. Copy outside the image (Meta/TikTok/LinkedIn)

For each ad, write the parts the platform shows around the image:

| Part | Meta guide | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Primary text | First ~125 characters show before "See more"; lead with the hook | Carry the angle; one proof; one CTA |
| Headline | ~27–40 characters visible | The benefit or the offer, not the brand name |
| Description | Often hidden; optional | Offer terms, shipping |
| CTA button | Shop now / Learn more / Sign up / Get offer / Download | Match the awareness stage (§2) |

LinkedIn: intro text ~150 characters visible, headline ~70; B2B buyers read more but skim the same way. TikTok: the caption is short (~100 characters visible); the image or video carries the hook.

Write two primary-text versions per ad (short punchy / longer story) so run-paid-campaigns can test copy without new images.
